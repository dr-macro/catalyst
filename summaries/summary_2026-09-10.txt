1) Bond markets are under strain as yields rerate and conventional playbooks break down.  
- $6bn U.S. Treasury buyback planned (Sept 10)  
- 10‑year U.S. yield at highest level since 2023  
- Market rejection of Bessent’s buyback; yields moved higher after announcement  
- UK gilt sale: highest borrowing cost since 1998  
- Global “$2tn” annual interest bill cited

2) Oil and Gulf security shocks are reintroducing supply risk and upside inflation pressure.  
- Brent/NYMEX crude around/above $100 per barrel  
- Saudi Aramco facilities hit in strikes; repeated Houthi attacks on Saudi cities  
- U.S. strikes on Iranian tankers; shipping via Strait of Hormuz below 10‑day average  
- EIA raised oil price forecasts; reports of a large share of Gulf oil “missing”

3) AI investment and activity are accelerating capital demand while prompting governance and credit questions.  
- Google to invest $15bn in Finland data‑centers/infrastructure  
- Mistral raised a record €3bn  
- Anthropic/OpenAI bankers pushing for top‑tier credit ratings post‑IPO  
- Anthropic disclosed a fourth hacking incident; OpenAI’s contested Navier‑Stokes claim reported  
- Private AI/tech valuations: Cognition $48bn; legal AI Harvey $15.5bn

4) Policy divergence and hawkish rhetoric are raising the odds of higher‑for‑longer real rates.  
- Reuters poll: Fed expected to hold rates steady through rest of 2026, though an increasing share forecast at least one hike  
- Fed Governor Waller urging patience even as yields signal tightening  
- RBA hawkish comments have raised expectations of a September hike  
- Commentary that sterling’s hawkish pricing may be overdone

5) Credit, leveraged and private markets face stress as spreads and funding costs adjust.  
- Historically tight credit spreads described as facing a test as rates rise  
- Offshore renminbi borrowing near record ~ $150bn  
- “Apollo premium” cited as raising debt costs for portfolio companies  
- Tether launches private credit fund; LIV Golf files for bankruptcy with ≥ $500mn liabilities

6) Risk assets are vulnerable to commodity‑driven volatility and worsening financing conditions.  
- U.S. stocks fell as oil spiked above $100; S&P finished down on the move  
- Fixed 30‑year mortgage rate climbed to highest since June 2025  
- Copper hitting multi‑year/seasonal highs; gold futures edging up despite higher U.S. yields

7) Geopolitical friction and protectionism are increasing near‑term policy and trade uncertainty.  
- U.S. imposes bans on many Canadian goods (dairy, motorcycles, most alcoholic beverages) and tariffs escalate  
- Canada implements retaliatory tariffs; calls for boycotts reported  
- EU and national measures shift procurement toward “Buy European”/tighter tender rules

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a higher‑real‑rates, supply‑shock sensitive regime: bond yields have repriced higher and liquidity is being tested while equities remain vulnerable to commodity and geopolitical shocks (notably oil). Policy is mixed—central banks cautious but markets pricing more tightening risk—so risk premia and credit funding costs are rising even as pockets of tech/AI froth continue to attract capital.
