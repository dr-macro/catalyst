1) Middle East security shock is driving an oil supply scare and spiking energy costs.  
  - Brent crude above $100/bbl multiple times (>$100, later >$102–$107 reported).  
  - Saudi Aramco facilities struck; Saudi output cut to its lowest this year.  
  - Houthis captured a Red Sea port (Bab el‑Mandeb); shipping via Strait of Hormuz and Bab el‑Mandeb below recent averages.  
  - US and allied strikes on Iranian-linked tankers; EIA raised oil-price forecasts; diesel >$6/gal in US.

2) Global bond markets are under sustained stress as central banks turn hawkish and yields rerate higher.  
  - ECB raised rates to 2.5% and signalled a new hiking cycle.  
  - 10‑year yields at multiyear highs / highest since 2023; global bond sell‑off described as reignited.  
  - US Treasury announced buyback up to $6bn (Sept 10); a $6bn buyback plan (Bessent) disappointed markets.  
  - UK paid highest gilt borrowing cost since 1998 at a sale; 30‑yr US mortgage topped ~7%.

3) Energy-driven inflation is feeding central‑bank hawkishness and near‑term growth/inflation uncertainty.  
  - Producer prices rose 0.4% in August; wholesale/producer prints surprised.  
  - Major central banks struck a more hawkish tone as energy costs jumped.  
  - Global coal demand forecast up 1.2% in 2026 to 8.94bn tonnes; clean‑hydrogen investment >$130bn.

4) AI boom accelerates corporate capex, fundraising and regulatory/safety scrutiny — creating concentration and systemic risk concerns.  
  - Google to invest $15bn in Finland AI data‑centre capacity; Nvidia/partners expanding data centres (Australia).  
  - Mistral raised a record €3bn; Anthropic/OpenAI/others pursuing IPOs and top credit ratings.  
  - Multiple AI safety incidents and researcher resignations disclosed; BIS flagged AI as a new financial‑stability risk.  
  - Banks and law firms mandate AI skills or curbs (UBS, law schools).

5) Geopolitical proliferation of advanced weapons and private defence startups is rising alongside state conflict.  
  - Reports of Russia aiding Iran on supersonic cruise missiles; Iran producing new missiles from existing components.  
  - Startups backed by major VC figures unveiling low‑cost/mass‑producible cruise/deep‑strike missiles.  
  - US strikes and escalating naval incidents; shipping/tanker attacks widespread.

6) China’s external surplus and tech/manufacturing moves reinforce strategic trade dynamics and supply‑chain reconfiguration.  
  - China’s exports surged ~25% with a growing trade surplus; China bought ~1m tonnes of US soybeans ahead of a state visit.  
  - Offshore renminbi borrowing hit a record near $150bn.  
  - TSMC/AI‑chip demand drove large revenue jumps (TSMC revenue +53% in August); AI chipmakers raising prices as HBM shortages bite.

7) Markets face a liquidity/credit stress test as corporate borrowing costs and private capital dynamics adjust.  
  - “Apollo premium” noted to be raising debt costs for portfolio companies; private equity debt pain highlighted.  
  - Credit spreads, historically tight, are being tested as rates rise.  
  - IPO/private‑market fundraising frenzy and shadow markets (DeepSeek) raising oversight concerns.

8) Politics and populism are adding policy unpredictability that could affect trade, fiscal and regulatory outcomes.  
  - Far‑right party surged to first place in a German state election; ECB/Bundesbank officials voiced concern.  
  - High‑profile US political moves: tariff measures and bans on certain Canadian goods; presidential campaign promises include $5,000 “dividends” and $500 Obamacare refunds for ~1m people.  
  - Trade tensions: proposed EU tariffs on Chinese cars, US–Canada trade friction; snap elections and political turbulence across Europe/Latin America.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a risk‑off turning point: oil‑led inflation and escalating geopolitical conflict have forced major central banks into tighter policy, driving a broad bond sell‑off and higher real yields that are testing equity valuations and credit markets. Simultaneously, an AI‑driven investment boom concentrates capital and systemic risk, raising regulatory and stability questions that amplify market volatility.
