1) Geopolitical escalation driving energy and shipping risk.
  - $40bn: Iran estimates windfall from Hormuz reopening.  
  - U.S. strikes reported; tanker struck and traffic through Strait of Hormuz slowed.  
  - Oil back to prewar levels; U.S. crude fell below $70.  
  - Mines and maritime risks to keep Strait shipping constrained for months.  
  - Insurers have cut war premiums for Strait of Hormuz routes.

2) Central-bank hawkishness and bond-market repricing; real yields rising.
  - Real yields rose above 2%.  
  - New Fed chair Warsh unveiled and tapped two veteran Fed economists as advisers.  
  - Front-end repricing risk flagged; long end described as anchored by hawkish sentiment.  
  - Gilts sensitive to political uncertainty.  
  - Global bonds stumbled as a surging US dollar added pressure.

3) Governments tightening control over frontier AI and model access on security grounds.
  - OpenAI deferred/limited public rollout of GPT‑5.6 / released to select vetted users.  
  - Anthropic models (Mythos/Fable) allowed to some trusted US orgs; US asked for staggered releases.  
  - Five Eyes warned AI-powered threats may succeed “within months.”  
  - EU and US joined a pact to reduce reliance on Chinese AI supply chains.  
  - Google limited Meta’s access to Gemini due to compute constraints.

4) AI-driven compute demand is amplifying semiconductor shortages and price pressure.
  - Samsung preparing a $648bn investment/bet tied to AI boom.  
  - Memory-chip cost rise prompted Apple to raise MacBook/iPad prices ~20% (about $200 on some models).  
  - Reports that the AI data-center boom is sparking a “third wave” of inflation and memory shortages.  
  - Micron and other chipmakers seen as key beneficiaries; chip valuations flagged as expensive.

5) Tech/space capital-structure stress and episodic market volatility.
  - SpaceX shed roughly $400bn of market value after an initial rally reversed.  
  - $25bn: SpaceX debt deal preceded bond sell‑offs.  
  - SpaceX set to join the Nasdaq‑100, implying large passive buying demand.

6) Rapid rearmament and defence-tech investment surge.
  - $12bn: venture capital flow into defence tech driven by wars.  
  - UK to commit £500mn to high-speed boats and drones for Commando Force.  
  - U.S. summoned munitions makers amid concerns over low stockpiles.  
  - Germany scrapped plans to build its biggest post‑WW2 warship.

7) Corporate restructuring, price rises and real‑economy shocks raising fiscal and labour risks.
  - VW plans up to 100,000 job cuts and four plant closures.  
  - Netherlands to open ~€1.5tn pensions market to foreign providers.  
  - Apple price increases ~20% / ~$200 on some Macs and iPads.  
  - Venezuela to disclose a $240bn debt pile as part of largest restructuring.  
  - Europe heatwave: electricity prices soared; France recorded ~1,000 excess deaths; Venezuela quake toll topped ~1,400.

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a higher‑volatility, multi‑risk regime: hawkish central-bank signalling and rising real yields / USD are pressuring global bond markets while geopolitical shocks (Middle East, maritime risks, Ukraine) are injecting episodic commodity and risk‑premia shocks. Simultaneously, an AI‑driven capex boom is fueling semiconductor demand and valuations, creating asymmetric upside in tech/assets tied to compute but broader tightening of financial conditions and defensive buying into bonds, defence and hedging assets.
