2) Bond-market repricing and higher real yields are increasingly constraining the equity rally and liquidity conditions.  
- Real yields near 20‑year highs.  
- Headlines: “Surging bond yields threaten to derail stock rally”; “Liquidity conditions begin to tighten as Treasury borrowing accelerates”.  
- Fed/markets warnings: more policymakers eye possible rate hikes.

3) US‑Iran kinetic exchanges and negotiations for a temporary ceasefire are driving elevated oil/energy volatility and market shocks.  
- US strikes Iranian missile sites; US‑Iran MOU/negotiations on 60‑day ceasefire pending Trump approval.  
- Oil: May fell ~20% from 2026 peak; oil falls >1% on ceasefire reports; Brent posts biggest monthly loss in six years.  
- Chevron warns prices likely to jump over summer; Exxon warns inventories will hit dangerously low levels.

- Fertiliser groups cut production as sulphur supplies squeezed.  
- Russia diesel output fell ~10% in May.  
- Cheniere signed $4.69bn deal with Bechtel to expand LNG exports.  
- Analysts repeatedly hike oil forecasts amid slow supply recovery expectations.

- Nine countries pulled out of Ukraine ammunition coalition.  
- Netanyahu ordered forces to take control of 70% of Gaza.  
- Russian drone struck Romanian apartment block; EU preparing fresh sanctions.  
- Poland and others fast‑tracking defence loans/contracts; Norway under France’s nuclear umbrella.

- EU to broaden import quotas and tariffs vs China; EU countries pushing trade crackdown on China.  
- EU Commission agreed to unlock €16.4bn for Hungary (policy conditionality context).  
- Proposals to raise North American auto content to 82% under USMCA reported.

7) Policy, wages and household strain: real wages contracting amid persistent core inflation and rising living costs.  
- Real wages starting to shrink in developed countries.  
- Core inflation (Fed’s preferred gauge) at 3.3% annual rate in April; key US inflation measure posts largest annual increase in three years.  
- Americans’ savings rate falling to lowest since 2022; average household paying ~$450 more on gas/energy from Iran war.

8) Corporate and market-structure shifts: big bank balance‑sheet capacity, M&A optionality, governance shocks and a volatile IPO pipeline.  
- Deregulation cited as handing top US/UK banks $1.3tn.  
- Jamie Dimon says JPMorgan could spend up to $20bn on acquisition.  
- Tilman Fertitta pursuing Caesars buyout (reports ~$18bn); BP chair removed amid boardroom scandal.  
- SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic IPO talk to ignite trading frenzy.

9) Labor, demographics and secular consumption trends (falling birth rates, GLP‑1 drugs, weak youth jobs) are reshaping demand and social policy.  
- Global birth rates falling (“everywhere all at once” coverage).  
- This summer’s teen job market described as toughest in decades.  
- France becomes first EU country to reimburse anti‑obesity drugs; GLP‑1 drugs discussed as retail/mall disruptors.

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
We’re in a risk‑on regime driven by an AI‑fuelled equity rally concentrated in semiconductors and data‑centre winners, but that optimism sits against tighter bond markets, rising real yields and episodic commodity/energy shocks from Middle East and supply disruptions. Policy uncertainty (rates, trade, defence) and liquidity tightening make gains fragile: markets are chasing tech growth while macro fundamentals and geopolitical risks increasingly set the bounds of downside.
