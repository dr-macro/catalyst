   - HPE shares surged ~37%; Marvell rallied ~32%; Nvidia launched a PC “superchip.”

2. Bond yields and real rates have moved materially higher, creating tightening liquidity and policy risk for risk assets.
   - Real yields near 20‑year highs.
   - Treasury borrowing acceleration coinciding with tightening liquidity.
   - Multiple bond‑market commentaries warning of more upside to rates.

3. Middle East hostilities around Iran are triggering an energy shock and elevated tail‑risk for growth and inflation.
   - Oil prices up; ADNOC planning UAE pipeline to bypass Strait of Hormuz.
   - Reports of IRGC attacks on US Fifth Fleet facilities and US strikes near Hormuz.
   - OECD/IEA warn of risk of critically low stockpiles and a protracted energy shock.

4. Central banks are responding to sticky inflation and tighten‑leaning data, increasing the odds of further rate hikes.
   - Eurozone inflation rose to 3.2% in May; ECB expected to hike in June and likely again in September.
   - Fed Chair Powell warned the Fed is undergoing a “stress test”; Fed officials say rates may need to rise if inflation persists.

5. Macro data show resilient labour markets but early signs of squeezed household incomes and falling real wages.
   - US job openings at 7.6m; ADP private payrolls +122k; private payrolls broadly rose in May.
   - Headlines: real wages starting to shrink in developed countries.

6. Market structure risks are growing as IPOs, megacaps and leveraged products concentrate flows and narrow breadth.
   - Vanguard ETF became first ETF to top $1tn in assets.
   - Leveraged ETF assets doubled in two months.
   - S&P 500 posting multi‑week winning streaks amid “breadth paradox” warnings.

7. Supply‑chain and resource security moves are accelerating across chips, critical minerals and data‑centre capacity.
   - SoftBank pledged €75bn for a major AI facility in France.
   - AI projected to double data‑centre power and water consumption by 2030.
   - USA Rare Earth to invest $1.2bn in South Carolina; China seeks US tungsten; EU seeks powers to seize chip supplies in crisis.

8. Private credit and liquidity strains are surfacing in alternative finance, prompting investor redemptions and caps.
   - Cliffwater flagship private credit redemption requests hit 17%.
   - Partners Group capped investor withdrawals, prompting share declines.

9. Policy and regulatory actions are proliferating across trade, AI and tech sovereignty as governments react to strategic risks.
   - US proposing tariffs of ≥10% on goods from 60 economies over forced‑labour concerns.
   - Trump signed an AI executive order seeking early government access to models; US asked AI firms to voluntarily submit models for cybersecurity tests.
   - EU planning broader import quotas/tariffs vs China and tech sovereignty measures (French search engine pilot, data‑centre energy standards).

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
We’re in an AI‑led risk‑asset upswing driven by concentrated megacap and IPO flows, running into rising bond yields, tighter liquidity and an energy shock from Middle East hostilities — a growth/inflation mix that raises policy uncertainty and tail‑risk. Market internals are fragile: narrow breadth, elevated single‑name concentration and mounting private‑credit/liquidity stresses increase downside dispersion and event risk.
