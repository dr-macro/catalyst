1. Monetary policy and rates are front-and-center as the Fed signals patience while a new chair nomination tilts markets toward balance-sheet tightening and higher long-term yields.  
   - Fed signals no rush to cut rates  
   - Kevin Warsh nominated/selected (Jan 30)  
   - Dollar jumped by the most since May 2025 on Warsh nomination  
   - Longer-term yields rose, short-term yields fell after Warsh pick  
   - “Yields have spiked but levels are far from overly elevated”

2. The yield-curve dynamic is shifting toward steepening and cross-border transmission, raising global fixed-income repricing risk.  
   - “Steeper curves still path of least resistance”  
   - Japanese yields are catching up to global moves  
   - Foreign bonds have led US fixed income in 2026  
   - ECB outlook “getting more complicated”

3. Fiscal expansion and higher borrowing are a primary market driver, particularly in EM and sovereign wealth moves.  
   - India to borrow a record 17.2 trillion rupees (FY2027)  
   - India raises infrastructure spending to 12.2 trillion rupees (2026–27)  
   - India gives 20-year tax holiday to GIFT City businesses  
   - Abu Dhabi folds assets worth $263bn into new wealth fund

4. An AI-driven capital frenzy is fueling massive fundraising and corporate investment while investor scrutiny and megadeal pauses create volatility.  
   - Anthropic doubles VC fundraising to $20bn  
   - Amazon in talks to invest up to $50bn in OpenAI  
   - Nvidia/OpenAI $100bn megadeal put on ice  
   - Microsoft shed ~$360bn in market value amid AI spending concerns  
   - ASML logs record orders; Waymo finalises $16bn round at $110bn valuation

5. Precious metals experienced extreme flows — a blowoff rally into late January followed by one of the worst intraday routs in decades.  
   - Gold soared past $5,000 (late Jan)  
   - Comex gold ended month $4,713.90 (up 8.98% for month)  
   - Silver plunged 31% in one session (second-worst day on record)  
   - Metals rout erased about $7 trillion (reported wipeout)

6. Shadow credit and private-finance risks have resurfaced after opaque trades and headline losses stoked fears about hidden leverage.  
   - Private credit firms selling debt to themselves at a record rate  
   - High-profile loss in private credit reopened scrutiny of hidden exposures

7. Geopolitical tensions — especially around Iran, the Gulf and Ukraine — are elevating risk premia and complicating alliance support and energy flows.  
   - Iran delegates emergency powers; Tehran warns of regional conflict  
   - Key Gulf allies say they won’t aid US in an Iran strike  
   - US links security guarantees for Ukraine to a peace deal ceding territory  
   - Russian oil income drops sharply under sanctions; OPEC+ keeps planned March pause

8. Risk sentiment and market structure are choppy: tech earnings and AI spending spooked investors, layoffs mount, and crypto/metals saw sharp moves.  
   - Big US companies set to lay off at least 52,000 workers  
   - Dow to cut ~4,500 employees (company-specific)  
   - US long-term unemployment hits a four-year high  
   - Bitcoin slipped below $80,000 (~$79k reported)  
   - Stocks and futures fell after Fed pick and metals rout

9. Currency and EM flows are re-pricing: a softer dollar has boosted EM activity and renewed talk of reserve-currency competition.  
   - Dollar fell to a four-month low (Jan 26) before jumping on Fed news  
   - Xi calls for the renminbi to attain global reserve status  
   - Yen experienced a sharp leap amid dollar moves  
   - Emerging markets made a strong start to 2026 as the dollar slid

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in a higher-for-longer, fiscally active environment where monetary restraint, large government/sovereign funding plans and AI-driven private capex coexist with elevated geopolitical risk. Market volatility is elevated — rates and cross-border bond flows lead, safe-haven/commodity episodes are frequent, and hidden credit stresses and tech-driven repricings create asymmetric tail-risk.
