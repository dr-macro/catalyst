1) AI supercycle: massive corporate and infrastructure spending is driving market concentration and creating systemic exposure across banks, cloud providers and chip suppliers.  
  - AI spending tops $1tn  
  - Amazon completes $50bn investment in OpenAI; Amazon increases AI infra spending to $220bn this year  
  - Microsoft added a record $450bn in market cap in one day  
  - Nvidia bets $5bn on Ilya Sutskever; Nvidia revealed as tenant for a $50bn data centre  
  - Banks in talks to lend ~$15bn for an Anthropic data centre

2) Fragile leverage and a fast unwind in AI-related positions has forced fire-sales and sponsor rescues, amplifying market volatility.  
  - Hedge funds face collateral demands as AI stocks tumble  
  - Situational Awareness fund plunged 67% in July  
  - Citadel bought most of Situational Awareness’s stock holdings / portfolio; reported as stemming a broader AI rout

3) Bond market repricing has pushed short-term rates higher, dented central bank credibility and left markets jittery about the Fed’s next move.  
  - 2‑year Treasury yield nearing a key breakout  
  - US borrowing costs reached a 19‑year high  
  - Fed held rates but three officials voted to increase; officials warned on credibility after the bond sell‑off

4) Energy shock and Middle East escalation are tightening physical supply, lifting prices and boosting oil majors’ profits — feeding through to inflation and rates.  
  - US oil inventories fallen to “precariously low” levels  
  - Oil prices up, posting the strongest month since March and settling >1% higher on a session cited  
  - Exxon/Chevron profits surged; Chevron posted highest quarterly profit in six years  
  - Tankers attacked / VLCCs exited the Strait of Hormuz; Saudi announced a maritime defence coalition

5) FX and intervention risks have risen as authorities defend disorderly moves in the yen and other currencies.  
  - Yen jumped ~3% amid intervention speculation  
  - Japan may have sold up to $59bn in yen‑buying intervention  
  - US Treasury signalled it may intervene to support the yen and later intervened after Japan stepped in

6) Geopolitical and migration shocks are broadening market tail risks and prompting policy responses across Europe and the US.  
  - Spain recorded ~49,000 migrants into Ceuta in 24 hours; reports referenced flows of ~60,000 people  
  - US and allies carry out/plan strikes and operations related to Iran‑linked escalation; energy‑related targets discussed  
  - Russia continued strikes in Ukraine, targeting energy infrastructure

7) China / global tech competition and policy shifts are creating a volatile backdrop for EM and supply chains.  
  - Chinese stocks on track for worst month in a decade  
  - Beijing pledges faster fiscal/credit spending to support the economy  
  - Chinese military researchers reportedly used US AI models to train defence systems; US barred imports from 43 more companies over forced labour concerns

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a high-volatility, multi‑risk regime driven by a rapid AI funding cycle that is colliding with higher real yields and geopolitically‑driven energy shocks. Markets are sensitive to short‑end rates and liquidity; policymakers are on the defensive—using FX intervention and mixed central‑bank signalling—while concentrated tech positions create outsized tail risk for credit, prime‑brokerage and hedge‑fund plumbing.
