1) AI boom meets rising regulation, model access limits and competition, creating a tapering-of-euphoria / vetting phase for frontier models.
   - $34bn spent by OpenAI last year
   - GPT‑5.6 released to select/vetted users; public rollout deferred
   - OpenAI asked to stagger new-model releases / delaying IPO discussions
   - JPMorgan cut Anthropic access for Hong Kong staff
   - Anthropic accuses Alibaba of illicit access; Anthropic hiring spree and veterans’ spinouts

2) AI infrastructure and memory-chip cycle is driving a volatile hardware boom and “chipflation” across tech supply chains.
   - Micron: 15‑fold profit surge; briefly overtook Meta and Tesla in market value
   - Micron + Qualcomm forecasts sparked ~$400bn AI chip stock rally
   - Nvidia‑banned chips doubled in price on China black market
   - SK Hynix $29bn US listing planned
   - Apple raised Mac/iPad prices by ~20% (some models +$200+); Xbox/console price increases cited due to memory costs
   - IBM unveiled tech for sub‑1nm chip

3) Inflation remains stickier than hoped, keeping markets on edge about further Fed hawkishness and front‑end repricing.
   - May PCE inflation topped 4% (headline) and core PCE 3.4% (May)
   - Fed signals plan to slow rate cuts; senior Fed/Warsh commentary leaning hawkish
   - Real yields rose above 2% in parts of the curve
   - ECB facing eurozone inflation climb, paving way for a rate hike

4) Middle East disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz are producing outsized energy and shipping risk despite temporary resumption of flows.
   - Cargo ship hit near Oman; oil rose ~2% after the strike
   - Oil returned to pre‑war levels; crude shipments at highest since the Iran war began
   - Iran estimates ~$40bn windfall from Hormuz reopening
   - Saudi Aramco resumed loadings at Ras Tanura after ~four‑month halt
   - UN paused escort plan after attack; US subsequently struck Iran

5) Market regime shows rapid sector rotation and heightened volatility as investors reprice tech, bid healthcare/defense and move into bonds.
   - US equity funds recorded weekly outflows (first since March)
   - Bond ETF flows surged as yields repriced; gilts hit by political uncertainty
   - SpaceX shed ~$400bn in market value after debut rally reversed
   - S&P/Nasdaq set for weekly falls on chip/tech weakness; investors most pessimistic since 2023 in some surveys
   - Healthcare and select industrials became havens while memory/AI hardware names swung violently

6) Defence spending and war dynamics are accelerating capital flows into defence tech and big-ticket programs.
   - Lockheed Martin landed a $35bn missile‑defence deal
   - ~$12bn venture‑capital rush into defence tech
   - Ukraine using longer‑range munitions (ATACMS) and intensified drone campaigns
   - NATO and allies preparing billions in new defence contracts / bank plans

7) Strategic onshoring and industrial policy (China, US, EU) are reshaping supply chains for chips, rare earths and AI hardware.
   - Plans for a Chinese state‑owned rare‑earths giant announced
   - EU/US pact to reduce reliance on Chinese AI supply chains
   - China detained two Japanese nationals over alleged rare‑earth smuggling
   - TSMC eyes multibillion‑dollar Arizona expansion; Samsung reported planning ~$648bn in investments

8) Physical shocks and climate extremes are adding policy and operational stress — from heatwaves to large earthquakes.
   - Europe set June temperature records (Britain, Switzerland)
   - Venezuela twin earthquakes killed dozens/hundreds; thousands missing and widespread damage
   - Heatwave strained grids; air‑conditioning and storage projects noted as investment themes

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in an AI‑capex led, high‑uncertainty regime: strong demand for AI compute is driving a volatile hardware cycle and heavy capex, but sticky inflation and hawkish central‑bank rhetoric keep real yields elevated and rate risk front‑and‑center. Geopolitical shocks (Hormuz/Iran, Ukraine) and climate/real‑world events are amplifying sector dispersion, pushing capital into defence, healthcare and bond hedges while tech leadership faces rapid reassessment.
