1. Bond-market rout and sharply higher yields are re-shaping risk pricing.
   - 10-year Treasury yield: hits multi‑decade highs (reported as 19-year and 24-year highs).
   - 30-year Treasury yield: highest since 2002.
   - Treasury market size concentration: hedge funds hold a record share of the $30 trillion Treasury market.
   - Repo/reverse‑borrowing surge and implied volatility rising on Treasuries.

2. AI investment boom is driving massive corporate capital deployment, buybacks and borrowing while triggering safety and regulatory friction.
   - Nvidia: launches $150bn share buyback.
   - Nvidia: turning to insurers to offset AI build‑out risks.
   - AMD: acquisitions ~ $8.0bn and $8.2bn (two deals reported).
   - OpenAI: annualized recurring revenue near $70bn; targets $30bn funding at ~$1.4tn valuation.
   - Anthropic: IPO filing warns of “existential risks”; $518bn AI build‑out cited as dependent on deals.
   - Regulators/investigators: FTC/open probes; top researchers call for oversight; OpenAI axes/delays new model releases over safety.

3. Energy and shipping shocks are creating acute diesel and crude market stress and near‑term supply policy actions.
   - Oil tanker spot rates: record $1.2mn per day.
   - Strategic releases/loans: US to loan up to 40 million barrels from SPR.
   - Diesel constraints: Russia extends diesel export ban until end‑October; White House urges drawdown of diesel inventories; US oil industry warns diesel prices may not normalize for a year.
   - Middle East flows: Saudi resumes Yanbu/Red Sea loadings; gulf shipments remain constrained.

4. Geopolitical volatility — Middle East, Russia and shifting U.S. posture — is amplifying market uncertainty.
   - US forces exit Iraq after two decades.
   - Political stance: Trump rejects Iran ceasefire proposal to reopen Strait of Hormuz.
   - Security incidents: Somali pirates killed 5 crew; UK links Iran to suspected airbase incident; Russia issues extreme threats over Kaliningrad.
   - Diplomatic signals: Belgium leak — US under Trump “no longer a reliable ally” (reported).

5. Credit, leverage and market-structure risks are rising as investors chase AI/growth while liquidity strains widen.
   - Private/public credit swings: pension funds seen selling ~$33bn in stocks; private credit and repo activity elevated.
   - Large corporate financing: Tesla lines up ~$30bn credit lines; KKR/BoE warn of AI‑related borrowing risks.
   - IPO/backlog: multiple IPOs postponed or slowed (Oura, others); markets seeing increased issuance pressures.

6. Data‑centre build‑out brings local payouts, contractual quirks and mounting political pushback ahead of elections.
   - Local incentives: households offered $10,000 in one town if a data centre is built.
   - Contracts: data‑centre investors could be paid even if sites lack electricity (Oracle deal detail).
   - Politics/regulation: US Senate blocks a data‑centre cost report/bill; industry lobbying to blunt local backlash; projects halted over permits in Indonesia.

7. Big-ticket corporate/defence deals and M&A are proceeding amid higher funding costs, squeezing non‑core players.
   - Defence awards: Boeing/Pentagon ~$20bn stealth‑fighter contract; Raytheon $20.7bn missile production deal.
   - Chemicals M&A: BASF bid ~€10.3bn for Evonik (rejected).
   - Energy/capex projects: Dangote $16bn Kenyan refinery (construction flagged/blocked in court reports); AAR $1.8bn stake deal; Burford could gain up to $1.4bn from Apple patent outcome.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a higher‑for‑longer real‑rates regime with multi‑decade high Treasury yields and elevated volatility, where energy‑supply shocks and geopolitics are adding inflation and risk premia upside. Markets are bifurcated: equities remain concentrated in AI/growth winners driving outsized capex and credit demand, while broad risk assets and credit-sensitive sectors are being repriced as funding costs and regulatory scrutiny rise.
