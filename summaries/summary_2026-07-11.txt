1) Geopolitical risk is elevated and directly pressuring energy and shipping routes, raising supply‑chain and commodity risk premia.
   - Strait of Hormuz: reported closure by IRGC navy; tanker traffic near standstill.
   - Iran/US: US strikes on Iranian railway bridges; Iran fired 10 ballistic missiles at Jordan's Azraq; US issues fresh Iran‑related sanctions.
   - Russia/Ukraine: Ukraine damaged 21 Russian tankers; Saratov refinery halted after drone attack; Russia gasoline output covers ~65% of domestic demand after strikes.
   - Market impact signals: IEA warns petrol/diesel supply crunch; strategic reserve buying to support crude demand through 2028.

2) Western rearmament and expanded defence commitments are becoming a sustained fiscal and industrial force.
   - Germany to borrow €800bn for rearmament.
   - Europe’s rearmament drive sustains ~195,000 US defence jobs.
   - NATO/Ukraine: new long‑range command and political backing to strike deeper into Russia.

3) AI is a two‑edged growth impulse — rapid corporate capex and hiring but rising costs, regulatory/legal friction and concentration risks.
   - Companies rein in AI usage as costs strain budgets; heavy AI spenders add staff faster than peers.
   - Data‑centre strain: Carlyle sold a $2.6bn data‑centre power unit; reports of a “data centre cover‑up” over capacity/power.
   - Legal/regulatory: Apple sues OpenAI alleging trade‑secret theft; OpenAI leadership and safety shakeups; China/US regulatory moves on models and chips.
   - Product moves: OpenAI unveiled a “super app”; Meta testing high‑sensing AI glasses.

4) Semiconductor/memory capital cycles are driving big equity flows and corporate investment, amplifying market dispersion.
   - SK Hynix US listing cited at ~$26.5bn and shares jumped ~13% on Nasdaq debut.
   - Micron commits $250bn of investment through 2035; Apple to spend $30bn on U.S.‑made chips from Broadcom; Nvidia supplier to invest up to $1.4bn in US facility.
   - China halts helium exports (input risk for chip fabs); CXMT spotlighted after Apple interest; SK Hynix warns worst memory shortage in 2027.

5) Inflation and rate volatility remain central macro risks as central banks react to supply shocks and fiscal shifts.
   - Fed officials fret over inflation risk and weigh rate hikes; analysts flag higher real rates and resumed curve steepening impulses.
   - Japan: borrowing costs at 30‑year high; calls for pension funds to invest more domestically.
   - Market signals: gold under pressure as Middle East tensions lift rate‑hike bets; investors selling longer‑dated AI debt amid Big Tech borrowing spree.

6) Energy/utility and climate stress are creating near‑term supply and infrastructure vulnerabilities.
   - UK/US power concerns: utility warnings of potential blackouts; Britain flagged “power problem”.
   - Europe heat and wildfires: heatwave, at least 12 dead in Spanish wildfire; Eurostar ordering trains built to endure 55°C summers.
   - Commodities: Russia diesel export ban deepens diesel crunch; IEA cuts Russian oil output forecasts after attacks.

Current macro/markets regime: risk assets are bifurcated — concentrated AI/semiconductor/capex winners drive equity upside while geopolitics and energy‑supply shocks keep volatility and inflationary upside live, forcing central banks toward vigilance; expect episodic spikes in oil, rates and safe‑haven flows amid ongoing political uncertainty.
