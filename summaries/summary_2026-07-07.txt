1) AI/data‑centre and semiconductor capex is driving an intense, concentrated rally but exposing valuation and supply‑chain stress.
  - Samsung: headlines point to an 18–19x profit surge and a third straight quarter of record profit.
  - SK Hynix: planning a US listing targeting ~$28bn.
  - Micron: supply tie‑ups with OEMs (vehicle agreement with Ford noted).
  - TeraWulf / Anthropic: $19bn data‑centre lease.
  - Chip stocks: KOSPI down nearly 5% after AI worries; broad chip selloffs following mixed earnings.

2) Geopolitical tensions (Iran, Russia‑Ukraine) and the NATO push are triggering a historic rearmament cycle and big defence contracts.
  - Germany: planned borrowing ~€800bn for rearmament.
  - NATO / Europe: rearmament sustaining ~195,000 US defence jobs; multiple multi‑bn defence deals (examples: $8bn maritime tech; Lockheed acquisition $3.45bn).
  - NATO members: several moving toward >3.5% of GDP defence spending; nine countries committed to a global defence bank.

3) Middle East maritime attacks and sanctions have reopened oil‑security risk, pushing short‑term oil volatility despite growing output.
  - Tanker incidents: ships and an LNG/Qatari gas tanker hit in the Strait of Hormuz; Saudi‑flagged tanker damaged.
  - Oil: prices moved back to pre‑Iran war levels but rose after attacks; markets are monitoring supply recovery and depleted stocks.
  - Policy: US revoked a licence authorising Iranian oil sales; OPEC+ output increases and UAE ADNOC LNG platform launched.

4) Deal activity and corporate financing are at cyclical highs, underwriting market liquidity and headline M&A.
  - Global dealmaking: record ~$2.8tn in transactions.
  - Amazon: bond offering seeking at least $25bn.
  - Major transactions: HSBC offer $13.6bn for Hang Seng; EasyJet takeover talks ~$7.3bn / £5.5bn; Solstice/Element ~$14.5bn.

5) Rates, bonds and FX are in a selective repricing: dollar strength and higher US yields are pressuring bonds and commodity/haven assets.
  - FX: yen pinned near 40‑year lows; dollar and yields rising.
  - Bonds: headlines show global bonds stumbling as a surging dollar increases pain and front‑end repricing risk; 10‑year Treasuries eyed for yield spikes.
  - Gold: pulled back from a two‑week high amid dollar/yield moves.

6) Regulators and banks are racing to close AI oversight gaps as firms scale up usage and costs trigger pullbacks.
  - Supervisory actions: top banking watchdogs and the ECB instruct banks to plan for AI cyber‑risks; UK regulator warns of an AI “arms race” in financial services.
  - Corporate responses: companies report rein‑in of AI usage due to cost strain; tech firms capping access (Google capped Meta’s Gemini use); Anthropic tightening access and being scrutinised by large customers.

7) Tech market dynamics show concentration risk: hyperscalers and a few chip names dominate sentiment while IPO/index moves add volatility.
  - SpaceX: Nasdaq‑100 inclusion and IPO attention; SpaceX shares volatile in debut.
  - Chip winners/losers: Samsung’s record profits failed to calm investors; Micron/memory mania questioned; traders betting on Nvidia while some AI‑related debt sells off.
  - Listings/IPO flows: SK Hynix US listing ~$28bn; major secondary offerings (e.g., Rivian share sale) affecting sentiment.

8) Energy security and critical‑minerals policy are rising policy priorities, prompting industrial investment and onshoring moves.
  - Minerals/EVs: US approved a lithium project to reduce China’s EV minerals grip; reports of corporate Japan warning on rare‑earth supply; Teck secured up to $400m for critical minerals expansion.
  - Industrial onshoring: substantial defence and data‑centre investments; large industrial capex plans (car production moves, new plant investments) noted.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
We are in an AI‑led growth bifurcation: concentrated equity upside around hyperscalers and selected chip/data‑centre beneficiaries while broader market breadth and cyclicals are driven by defence, energy‑security and commodity themes. A strong dollar and higher US yields are forcing bond/FX repricings and amplifying volatility, with geopolitical shocks (Hormuz, Russia‑Ukraine) keeping commodity and risk premia elevated.
