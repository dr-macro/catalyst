- $300bn wiped off software and data stocks  
- Hedge funds have made $24bn shorting software so far in 2026  
- AMD down 17% (worst day since 2017)  
- Arm down ~8% after results  
- Google to double AI spending to $185bn  
- Amazon preparing ~200bn AI spending (reported); capex +50% guidance reported  
- SpaceX/xAI tie-up forming a $1.25tn company

2) Bond markets and central banks are navigating higher yields, policy uncertainty and idiosyncratic political risk.  
- Bank of England held rates at 3.75% (5–4 vote)  
- ECB held rates at 2%  
- Eurozone inflation 1.7% in January  
- Fed has bought over $90bn in Treasury bills since December  
- Warnings that Japan’s yields are “catching up” and BOJ won’t backstop a Takaichi‑driven rout

3) Geopolitical tensions are intermittently spiking oil and defence flows while depressing some energy revenues.  
- Oil prices climbed ~3% ahead of US‑Iran talks in one report  
- US shot down an Iranian drone; US and Iran agreed to talks in Oman  
- Russia’s oil & gas revenue halved year‑on‑year in January (lowest since July 2020)  
- Cuba said to have 15–20 days of oil left  
- Pemex supplied $496m of oil to Cuba in 2025  
- India’s Reliance bought 2 million barrels of Venezuelan oil (reported)

4) Precious metals and commodity markets are experiencing extreme volatility and forced re‑positioning.  
- Silver plunged ~13% (other reports cited moves up to ~16%)  
- Chinese trading firm Zhongcai netted $500m from the silver rout  
- iShares Physical Metals issued 1.2m gold ETC securities  
- Gold traded around/through the $5,000/oz headlines (record/volatile moves reported)

5) Strategic industrial policy and the critical‑minerals race are becoming explicit national security instruments.  
- Senators pushed a $70bn funding deal to back the US critical‑minerals agenda  
- US proposed a critical‑minerals trade bloc / trade zone (aimed at countering China)  
- EU set to announce a major trade package with India; US–India trade deal reporting targets March

6) Labour and growth indicators point to cooling employment even as the economy avoids collapse.  
- Big US companies set to lay off at least 52,000 workers (reported)  
- US job cuts hit the highest January total since 2009 (headline)  
- Private payrolls rose only 22,000 in January (ADP)  
- US job openings fell to a more than five‑year low (reported)

7) Corporate funding and balance‑sheet activity remain heavy as firms finance AI capex and M&A.  
- Oracle raised $25bn in a bond offering and launched a $20bn at‑the‑market share program (and completed $25bn debt issuance)  
- Santander agreed $12.2–12.3bn deal to buy Webster Financial (cross‑border bank M&A cited)  
- Glencore/Rio merger talks abandoned on a $260bn deal (reported)

8) Tech supply chains and chip controls are shaping exports, national policy and investment flows.  
- Nvidia AI chip sales to China stalled by a US security review / export conditions reported  
- Nvidia’s sale to ByteDance contingent on Trump administration conditions (report)  
- TSMC plans ~$17bn investment in 3nm production in Japan

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a risk‑off, regime‑transition environment: real rates and yield volatility are elevated while equity markets are being repriced for rapid AI disruption and slowing labour momentum. Geopolitical flashpoints (Middle East, Russia, supply‑chain security) are intermittently moving commodity prices and safe‑haven flows, and corporates are leaning into large AI capex and financing programs even as credit and funding markets stay watchful.
