1) AI boom is driving outsized corporate revenues, huge capex commitments and violent sentiment swings that are re-rating tech and software.  
  - $68bn Q4 sales; first $200bn year (Nvidia)  
  - Big Tech ~ $650bn planned AI investment in 2026 (Bridgewater figure)  
  - Nvidia/OpenAI abandoned $100bn deal for a $30bn investment  
  - AMD–Meta multibillion chip deal > $100bn  
  - Amazon $50bn OpenAI investment reported as conditional  
  - Companies cutting jobs as they pivot to AI: C3.ai −26% headcount cut; Block plans ~40% cuts; WiseTech −33% workforce  

2) Markets are oscillating between AI euphoria and “implosion” fears, producing sector rotations and episodic software routs.  
  - Software and private-capital shares hit fresh waves of selling  
  - Investors pour record sums into European stocks (regional flows)  
  - Tech rallies but stocks sometimes fail to rally post-earnings (muted response to Nvidia beat)  
  - Options/volatility pricing: smallest post‑earnings swings priced in for Nvidia  

3) Geopolitical risk—especially US‑Iran—has spiked military presence and is feeding oil/commodity risk premia and safe‑haven demand.  
  - US gathered most air power in the Middle East since 2003  
  - Trump gave Iran 15 days ultimatum; US–Iran talks resumed in Geneva  
  - OPEC+ considering ~137,000 bpd output increase for April  
  - EIA showed a large US crude stock build; “oil on the water” at record levels  
  - Gold gains as tariff and geopolitical risks rise  

4) Trade and tariff policy uncertainty from the US is creating big legal and cash-flow shocks for corporates and markets.  
  - Trump’s flat-rate tariff took effect at 10%; later messaging/steps toward 15% reported  
  - Supreme Court decision blunted some tariff authority; US to stop collecting certain tariffs deemed illegal  
  - $130bn estimated race for companies to claim tariff refunds; court deadlines imminent  
  - Stocks and yields move on tariff uncertainty  

5) Credit and private‑markets stress is resurfacing, prompting redemptions scrutiny and accounting concerns.  
  - Blue Owl halted redemptions; private‑credit stocks slid  
  - Some private credit firms accused of using accounting tools to mask leverage  
  - Global government spending pushed gross debt to $348tn (IIF)  
  - US banks reported record ~$300bn profits in 2025 (but face cost/operational pressures)  

6) Bond markets remain central to price discovery as yields hover at elevated levels and investors reach for duration selectively.  
  - US 10‑year Treasury around 4.07% (pause noted)  
  - Bond investors “embrace maturity risk” and long-date pressures are rebuilding  
  - Treasury yields ease as tariff/geopolitical news reshapes the curve  

7) Energy, autos and critical‑minerals supply dynamics are causing large corporate write‑downs and strategic repositioning.  
  - Stellantis reported ~€20bn H2 loss after EV writedowns and warned of higher US tariff costs  
  - Volvo recall 40,000 EVs over battery fire risk  
  - Zimbabwe suspended lithium exports → lithium price jump  
  - Rare‑earth shortages worsening for aerospace/chips  

8) Corporate capital allocation and regulation are in flux—big buybacks, M&A frenzies and rising scrutiny on AI, data and subsidies.  
  - Rolls‑Royce buyback up to £9bn; LSE plans £3bn buyback  
  - Stripe valuation reported at $159bn; Stripe eyeing PayPal assets  
  - Meta–AMD chip deal and multiple multibillion AI/semiconductor contracts announced  
  - IMF urges China to halve industrial subsidies; EU states clash over subsidies and anti‑fraud probes increase  

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in an AI‑cycle-driven, risk‑on/risk‑off market where tech leadership and capital spending coexist with sharp sentiment volatility, elevated geopolitical tail risks (Middle East, Russia) and ongoing trade/tariff policy shock. Real rates remain high enough to keep bond markets central to positioning, while private‑credit and supply‑chain stresses (energy, critical minerals, EVs) raise asymmetric downside risks for credit and cyclical sectors.
