1) AI boom and messy rollout is reshaping corporate capex, competition and regulation.  
  - OpenAI spending: $34bn last year  
  - OpenAI IPO: likely delayed until next year (leaning toward waiting)  
  - Custom chips: OpenAI/Broadcom and Broadcom/OpenAI tie-ups; Qualcomm developing data‑centre chips; Qualcomm forecasts $15bn DC chip sales by 2029  
  - Chip market moves: Micron’s blowout (15-fold profit surge; >$200bn market value added) and SK Hynix $29bn US listing plan  
  - Security/controls: Anthropic model found vulnerabilities to classified systems; JPMorgan cut Anthropic access for Hong Kong staff; allegations Anthropic/Alibaba illicit access; Five Eyes warns AI-powered threats may succeed “within months”  
  - Cost pushback: companies rein in AI use as costs strain budgets

2) Central bank hawkish repricing is re-setting bond markets and lifting real yields.  
  - Fed inflation gauge: PCE >4.0% in May; core PCE 3.4%  
  - Real yields: rose above 2% in recent trading  
  - Dollar: strengthened to 13‑month/one‑year highs  
  - Market signals: bond-market positioning described as bracing for hawkish ECB and repricing front end risks

3) Oil/energy market swings driven by Strait of Hormuz developments and rising Gulf flows have removed the wartime premium.  
  - Brent: settled at lowest since before start of Iran war; oil back to prewar levels; U.S. crude briefly dipped below $70/bbl  
  - Tankers/shipments: nearly 1,200 cargo ships were at one point stranded with ~$125bn of goods; crude shipments through Hormuz now at highest since war began  
  - Insurance/pricing: war premiums on Strait of Hormuz routes slashed; global physical crude markets signaling near‑term oversupply

4) Semiconductor and AI‑infrastructure megacap re-rating is a core market driver and source of volatility.  
  - Micron: 15x profit surge; stock added >$200bn in value; blockbuster earnings revived AI trade  
  - SK Hynix: targeting ~$29bn U.S. listing / Nasdaq debut  
  - Qualcomm: acquisition of Modular ~ $3.9–4.0bn; forecasts and wins sparked chip rally (~$400bn AI chip-stock lift across names)  
  - Nvidia: restricted AI chips doubled in price on China black market

5) Geopolitical conflict and rearmament are boosting defence demand and government fiscal asks.  
  - VC into defence tech: $12bn rush into startups post‑wars  
  - U.S. supplemental requests: White House sought $87.6bn for Iran war, farm aid and related items  
  - Major defence contracts: Lockheed/THAAD noted >$35bn deals and munitions re‑stocking talks with industry

6) Market heat and idiosyncratic corporate events are spiking volatility and risk dispersion.  
  - SpaceX: post‑IPO selloff erased ~$400bn of market value; pitched a $25bn bond deal to investors  
  - Meme/retail moves: Wendy’s surged >25% in meme rally; Bitcoin slid to a 20‑month low  
  - Equity flows: bond ETF flows surged as investors hunt yield; Nasdaq futures swung +2% on chip names after Micron

7) Inflation remains sticky and is translating into tighter real rates and higher borrowing costs for consumers.  
  - PCE: headline >4.0% (May), core 3.4%  
  - Mortgage: 30‑year rate edged up to 6.49% (Freddie Mac report)  
  - Labor/consumption: US weekly jobless claims fell; consumer spending described as strong amid mixed growth signals

8) Climate/utility stress and physical risks are surfacing as tangible near‑term economic costs.  
  - Heatwave impacts: major European power outages (France), nuclear plant curbs, hospitals cancelling operations; hundreds of thousands of poultry killed in France  
  - Corporate demand: air‑conditioning and data‑centre cooling stocks seeing strong demand

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
Central banks are being forced to price more tightening as inflation readings stay above target, lifting real yields and the dollar; markets are rotating from broad tech growth into AI‑infrastructure, semiconductor winners and defence, producing episodic volatility (IPO selloffs, meme rallies) while geopolitical risk and energy flows create short‑term commodity swings. The net is higher rates, higher dispersion, and elevated policy/geopolitical tail risks.
