1. AI frenzy is driving massive corporate spend, M&A and regulatory pushback while warnings grow of an investment bubble.
- $200bn M&A boom in US power sector tied to AI
- AWS commits $1bn to new AI unit
- OpenAI releases GPT‑5.6 to select users vetted by US government
- BIS and other officials warn of “AI exuberance” and risk of lengthy investment bust
- Companies rein in AI usage as costs strain budgets

2. An escalating chip mega‑buildout is concentrating capex into South Korea and memory supply chains.
- Samsung & SK Hynix plan ~$600bn chipmaking expansion (multiple reports: $520bn–$600bn cited)
- South Korea AI‑chip drive: ~$576bn package
- CXMT memory supply deal: $3bn with Tencent

3. Middle East strikes and Strait of Hormuz disruption have reintroduced acute oil and shipping risk that is already moving markets.
- US–Iran exchanges and strikes around Hormuz; shipping evacuations halted
- Oil briefly back to pre‑war levels; US oil > $70/barrel at points
- US Strategic Petroleum Reserve down ~5.5m barrels to lowest since 1983

4. Market leadership is rotating: mega‑cap tech volatility, high‑yield financing strains and eye‑watering corporate bond/bank activity.
- “Magnificent Seven” shed ~$2.3tn in market value in short rotation
- SpaceX share slide removed ~$400bn of market value after debut rally; $25bn SpaceX bond sale noted
- Borrowed money fuelling rally getting more expensive; real yields rise above 2%

5. FX dislocation and central‑bank divergence: a hawkish Fed tone, strong dollar and yen at intervention risk.
- Japanese yen at 40‑year low (multiple headlines)
- Real yields >2%
- ECB: “does not need to fight inflation with same force as 2022‑23” (Lagarde)

6. Defence and geopolitical rearmament are pulling private capital and VC into the sector at scale.
- Wars have triggered ~$12bn of venture capital into defence tech
- Europe rearmament sustaining ~195,000 US defence jobs
- Saab / Gripen deal: ~$2.54bn; UK defence plan: £5bn for drones

7. Energy, power and climate stresses are raising short‑term supply risks for Europe and utilities.
- Europe could start winter with gas stocks at 15‑year low
- Electricity prices soaring during heatwave; freight and LNG flows disrupted
- Airlines face up to $127bn in extra costs from carbon credit shortage (industry estimate)

8. Corporates are aggressively cutting costs and reshaping workforces amid competition and higher input costs.
- Volkswagen to axe up to 100,000 jobs
- British American Tobacco cuts ~9,000 roles
- Apple raises MacBook and iPad prices by ~20%

9. Trade, tech sovereignty and export controls are accelerating supply‑chain bifurcation.
- China places 20 Japanese entities on export‑control list
- EU sets October deadline to reduce trade deficit with China
- EU joins US pact to reduce reliance on Chinese AI supply chains

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a hawkish, risk‑selective regime: higher real yields and a strong dollar are pressuring long-duration assets while an AI‑driven reallocation fuels both explosive tech capex and bubble warnings. Geopolitical shocks (Middle East, Russia‑Ukraine) and supply‑chain decoupling are lifting defence, energy and industrial risk premia even as central banks signal more cautious communication and policy divergence.
