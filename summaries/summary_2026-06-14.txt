1) Central banks and rising supply are tightening financial conditions and pressuring bond markets.  
  - ECB raised interest rates on 2026-06-11 (first time since 2023).  
  - Bank of Japan set to hike to a 31-year high (reported 12 Jun).  
  - Treasury borrowing has accelerated, tightening liquidity (headline 2026-05-25).

2) Oil and Middle East developments are driving commodity volatility but near-term peace signaling has cut risk premia.  
  - Brent fell to its lowest since March (Reuters 12 Jun).  
  - Oil touched a three-month low (FT 12 Jun).  
  - Reports a US-Iran peace deal text circulated (Pakistan/other sources) while Iran said no final decision; US shot down Iranian attack drones (Reuters 13 Jun).  
  - Iran said about 20,000 people were left without water after US strikes on reservoir tanks (10 Jun).

3) The SpaceX IPO reshaped market flows, generating enormous demand and dramatic valuation moves across tech/space stocks.  
  - IPO priced at $135, raising $75 billion (reported 12 Jun).  
  - Market cap surged past $2 trillion on debut.  
  - At least $5 billion order from BlackRock; intense retail/platform demand reported.  
  - Space/mega-tech stocks rallied then cooled; some proxies plunged post-debut.

4) AI national-security concerns and regulatory scrutiny are disrupting model access and accelerating competitive pressure and price competition.  
  - NSA reported to have used Anthropic’s Mythos for cyber operations (4 Jun).  
  - U.S. order limiting foreign access led Anthropic to disable top-tier models, including Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (reported 13 Jun).  
  - OpenAI under multi-state investigation and reportedly considering drastic price cuts amid an emerging AI price war.  
  - U.S. bank regulators and other authorities ramping up AI scrutiny.

5) Liquidity stress showed up in private-credit and redemption frictions, highlighting non-bank funding vulnerability.  
  - BlackRock private-credit fund honored less than 40% of redemption requests (FT).  
  - Separate report: the fund faced ~13% redemption requests (WSJ).

6) Markets are positioned for more persistent/hawkish policy as inflation and “sticky” rate narratives persist despite some contrarian views.  
  - Multiple “Rates Outlook” pieces highlighted sticky circumstances and continued hawkish risk.  
  - Gold sank to a six-month low and headed for a second weekly loss as speculative investors exited.  
  - Social Security COLA estimates implying higher prices (COLA projection ~4.7% for 2027 reported).

7) Geopolitical and defence dynamics are straining supply chains and defence procurement, reshaping regional risk premia.  
  - US plans cuts to fighter jets/warships for NATO operations reported; Lockheed cannot commit Patriot delivery timings.  
  - European defence stock rally cooled on funding concerns; Diehl in talks to build Ukrainian missiles in Germany.  
  - Oil tanker “dark” transits through Strait of Hormuz increased.

8) China/tech tensions and large corporate/regulatory actions are influencing capital flows and market structure.  
  - China cancelled high-level meetings with the EU; Beijing protested US moves against top Chinese tech firms.  
  - Nvidia began Vera CPU sales pitches to Chinese clients (reported 12 Jun).  
  - OpenAI and Anthropic regulatory actions prompted market and platform responses.

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a higher-for-longer rates regime with tightening liquidity driven by accelerated sovereign supply and central-bank tightening, while risk appetite is fragmented — volatile commodity and geopolitical shocks (Middle East, defence) intermittently shift risk premia. Simultaneously, massive tech/AI and mega-IPO flows (SpaceX) are creating concentrated equity volatility and putting non-bank liquidity and regulatory risks (AI, private credit) into focus.
