1) Bond markets and real yields have taken center stage as higher real rates and faster Treasury supply tighten liquidity and reshape risk pricing.  
   - real yields near 20-year highs  
   - Treasury borrowing accelerating; liquidity conditions beginning to tighten  
   - repeated "rates outlook" warnings that markets remain exposed to more upside

2) Strong US jobs and hawkish Fed signalling have pushed rate-hike bets higher and dented safe-haven assets.  
   - US posted a third straight month of strong job growth; resilient May payrolls cited  
   - investors boosted bets for a Fed rate rise; rate-futures lift odds for December hike  
   - gold fell about 3% after robust US jobs data

3) Energy shocks from the Middle East are draining inventories, forcing rerouting plans and creating acute market strain.  
   - US oil stocks at lowest level since 2004  
   - Gulf producers planning pipelines to bypass Strait of Hormuz (ADNOC, other Gulf talks)  
   - warnings of an oil market "inflection point" and depleted global inventories

4) AI and cloud compute are driving a fresh wave of mega-capraisings, IPOs and state interest — but also governance and safety debates.  
   - Alphabet to sell $80bn in stock to fund AI spending  
   - Anthropic filed to IPO and expands Mythos to >15 countries; Anthropic urges global pause on AI development  
   - Google/SpaceX compute deals reported: ~$920m/month and ~$30bn lease discussions; governments exploring equity stakes in AI firms

5) Tech and chip stocks are exhibiting extreme volatility as the AI trade concentrates capital and sparks sharp sector rotations.  
   - Broadcom suffered a $285bn rout after a disappointing outlook  
   - chip slump erased about $1.3tn in market value; Nasdaq tumbled ~4% on the sector hit  
   - Marvell and other chip/data‑center names seeing index moves (S&P additions)

6) SpaceX’s IPO frenzy highlights retail demand and valuation exuberance while index rules and market structure create frictions.  
   - SpaceX IPO reportedly ~2x oversubscribed for some tranches  
   - banks told not to move $135/share IPO price; S&P blocked early index entry  
   - Morgan Stanley projects SpaceX revenue of $3.4tn by 2040; UK/retail demand in the tens of thousands

7) Liquidity and redemption stresses are showing up in private credit and asset managers as investors test access to alternative assets.  
   - Blackstone caps withdrawals from flagship private credit fund  
   - Partners Group preparing/capping withdrawals at funds for wealthy investors  
   - DoubleLine/Oaktree reported buying debt ahead of potential AI-credit risks

8) Geopolitical and EM developments are driving capital flows and policy moves, from reserve-asset shifts to China/EM structural adjustments.  
   - ECB: gold has replaced US Treasuries as the world's top reserve asset (headline)  
   - Hong Kong overtook Switzerland as a hub for offshore wealth  
   - China: housing risks flagged; China banks raising dollar deposit rates amid yuan strength; industrial footprint expanding into Morocco

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):  
We are in a hawkish, liquidity‑constrained regime where rising real yields and heavy Treasury supply collide with energy‑driven inflation risk and elevated geopolitical tail‑risks; equities remain bifurcated — AI/cloud winners concentrating gains amid episodic sector crashes — while safe‑haven and reserve‑asset dynamics (gold vs Treasuries) are shifting. Markets are therefore sensitive to payroll prints, oil developments, and large AI/IPO capital moves.
