1) Middle East conflict and Strait of Hormuz blockade are the dominant geopolitical shocks driving market volatility and supply disruption.
- U.S. naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz announced and described as “fully implemented.”
- U.S. destroyer interdicted two oil tankers; six ships turned around as part of blockade.
- Iran demanding crypto fees for ships; Iran used a Chinese spy satellite to target U.S. bases.
- Damage estimates to energy infrastructure ≈ $58 billion (Rystad).

2) Oil and fuel markets face acute dislocation — big price swings, supply routings and refinery/jet-fuel stress.
- Oil spiked above $100/barrel; later volatile and retreating on talk hopes.
- IEA/IMF: global oil demand set to contract; IEA warns supply squeeze.
- European airports warn of jet-fuel shortages within ~3 weeks; airlines urging intervention.
- South Korea secured 273 million barrels via routes outside Hormuz; U.S. crude exports hit record highs.

3) Markets have bifurcated: equities (led by AI/tech) rally while energy and real-economy sectors show stress; trading desks are benefiting.
- S&P 500 hit intraday/record highs; tech names (Nvidia) on multi-day winning streaks.
- Wall Street banks to report ≈ $40 billion in trading revenue lift; several banks posted bumper trading quarters.
- Luxury names hit hard (Hermès down ~14%); LVMH and others report weaker sales.
- Gold and silver swung (gold up ~2% then pulled back); bitcoin > $74k in some headlines.

4) Bond markets and rate expectations are unsettled — short-end inflation expectations have risen and cut timing is disputed.
- Short-end inflation expectations surged, putting “rate cuts on ice.”
- Bond market pushing back on Fed inflation view; 10-year yield market premium likely widening.
- Risky debt outperformed Treasuries in wartime market conditions.
- Fed-politics noise: Treasury Secretary Bessent supports eventual cuts but Fed nominees/pressure noted; threats to remove Fed chair increased political risk.

5) AI, chip demand and cyber risk are central structural market themes supporting tech-capex and valuations — and attracting regulatory scrutiny.
- OpenAI valuation questioned at ~$852 billion; Anthropic attracting VC interest up to ~$800 billion.
- New AI models (e.g., GPT-5.4-Cyber, Anthropic Mythos) prompted regulator/central-bank briefings and cyber-risk discussions with senior officials.
- ASML raised 2026 outlook on surging AI chip demand; CoreWeave/Jane Street $6 billion AI-cloud deal; Meta extended custom-chip deals with Broadcom.

6) Europe and broader geopolitics are shifting: defensive rearmament, political turnover and new trade/energy alignments.
- Hungary’s opposition won, ending a 16-year government; EU ties €35 billion fund release to political change.
- NATO access and European defence cooperation under strain; major drone and defence orders (e.g., €300 million Rheinmetall).
- EU increasing purchases of Russian gas and debating industrial/antitrust responses; China using export controls and trade levers.

7) Real-economy pain and policy risks: inflation passes through to consumers and corporates, prompting fiscal/energy responses.
- Petrol > $4/gallon in U.S.; used-EV sales surge; truckers’ diesel spending at record highs.
- IMF warns global growth could slow to weakest since pandemic; IMF and EU flag financial-stability and fiscal risks.
- Governments taking ad hoc measures: Canada suspending federal fuel tax temporarily; UK offering payments to shift electricity use.
- Corporate actions: largest U.S. banks spent ~$33 billion on buybacks; major M&A/megadeals continue (Amazon–Globalstar ~$11.6 billion).

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in a regime of geopolitically driven commodity shock layered on a tech-led equity rally — risk assets have rallied on hopes of diplomacy while volatility and fragmentation persist across rates, FX and energy markets. Short-end inflation and political interference raise central-bank uncertainty, producing a bifurcated market where trading and AI capex outperform while real-economy sectors (energy, travel, luxury) and safe-havens remain sensitive to headline risk.
