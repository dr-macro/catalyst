1) AI-driven market repricing and big-tech consolidation.
  - $300bn wiped off software and data stocks.
  - Hedge funds have made $24bn shorting software stocks so far in 2026.
  - Anthropic employee share-sale plans at a ~$350bn valuation.
  - OpenAI–Nvidia mega-deal (~$100bn) reported stalled/“on ice.”
  - Nvidia nearing a ~$20bn investment in OpenAI.
  - SpaceX acquires xAI in a $1.25tn deal; share-exchange ratio set at 0.1433.
  - Oracle raises $25bn via bond offering.
  - Apple buys Israeli start-up Q.AI for close to $2bn.
  - Google adds ~$55bn to capital spending for AI.

2) Bond-market repricing and a Warsh-driven rates regime take shape.
  - President picked Kevin Warsh as Fed chair nominee.
  - Investors ramp up bets on a steeper yield curve under a Warsh Fed.
  - Headlines: yields “have spiked” though still “far from overly elevated.”
  - Japanese yields are catching up to global peers.
  - BOJ signalled it will not intervene to stop a Takaichi-driven bond rout.
  - US Treasury announced a $125bn refunding.

3) Middle East flare-ups are boosting oil and haven assets while diplomatic talks continue.
  - US shot down an Iranian drone.
  - Oil rose ~2% after the incident and has extended gains amid Iran tensions.
  - Gold recovered above $5,000/oz and posted its largest daily gain since 2008 on a rebound.
  - US and Iran scheduled/expected to hold nuclear talks (Oman/Istanbul), even as Iran delegates emergency powers.
  - Russia’s oil & gas revenue roughly halved year-on-year in January to its weakest since July 2020.

4) Trade deals and industrial policy are reshaping supply chains for critical minerals and energy.
  - US plans a $12bn critical-minerals stockpile.
  - Senators pushing a $70bn funding package for critical-minerals agenda.
  - US to cut tariffs on India to 18%; EU–India deal to slash ~€4bn of tariffs.
  - India to ramp up US oil and arms purchases; rupee posted its biggest one-day gain in seven years.
  - Some 30 countries seeking to join a critical-minerals club.

5) Large corporate M&A, buybacks and strategic deals remain active.
  - Santander agrees a ~$12.2bn takeover of Webster Financial.
  - KKR and Singtel to pay ~$5.2bn for remaining stake in STT GDC.
  - Texas Instruments in advanced talks to buy Silicon Labs for about $7–7.5bn.
  - UBS reports a ~$1.2bn fourth-quarter profit and plans a $3bn buyback.
  - Walmart’s market value reached ~$1tn.

6) Commodities and metals: violent repricing and return-to-haven flows.
  - Gold trading back above $5,000/oz.
  - Silver experienced a rout then rebound; one Chinese trading firm (Zhongcai) netted $500mn from the silver rout.
  - Base-metals calls tempered amid volatile positioning and supply concerns (notably copper).

7) Geopolitical realignment and defence/security spending are rising.
  - EU envoys agreed details of a €90bn loan package for Ukraine.
  - NATO started military planning for Arctic operations; Germany expanding space/missile detection capability.
  - Major defence firms winning large orders (deal flow described as “thousands” of missiles in new US contracts).

8) Labour and growth indicators showing early signs of cooling amid mixed consumer signals.
  - Big US companies announced plans to lay off at least ~52,000 workers.
  - ADP private payrolls rose by just 22,000 in January.
  - Eurozone inflation fell to 1.7% in January (below ECB target).
  - Consumer sentiment and discretionary-packaged goods seeing strains (multiple price/volume stories).

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in an volatility-prone transition: markets are re-pricing around an acceleration of AI-driven disruption and large balance-sheet moves by tech firms, while expectations for a more hawkish Fed tilt (Warsh) steepen yield curves and amplify bond market sensitivity. Geopolitical risk (Middle East, Ukraine, energy sanctions) is lifting commodities and safe havens even as trade and industrial-policy initiatives (critical minerals, tariffs) redirect capital into defense, mining and infrastructure.
