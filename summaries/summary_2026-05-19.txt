1) Middle East conflict is driving an energy shock that is tightening global fuel supplies and lifting inflationary pressure.  
- Oil climbed to two-week highs; oil inventories and commercial stocks reported rapidly depleting.  
- US strategic reserve draws left volumes at a two‑year low.  
- IEA: commercial oil inventories “depleting rapidly, only weeks left.”  
- Saudi Aramco: warned fuel stocks heading for “critically low levels.”  
- Iran war/global disruption estimated to saddle companies/consumers with ~$25–40bn in costs.  
- Ryanair suspended guidance after jet‑fuel surge; mortgage costs rising on Middle East conflict.

2) Bond markets are under sustained strain as yields reprice higher on broader inflation and energy risks.  
- Gilt yields moved to multi‑year/record highs.  
- 30‑year US Treasury yield topped ~5.18% (highest in nearly 19 years).  
- Headlines: “bonds extend sell‑off”; investors see ongoing bond market strain.  
- Foreign government holdings of US Treasuries fell (Japan, China leading retreat).

3) AI investment frenzy is reshaping corporate spending, valuations and competition across tech and services.  
- Big Tech AI spend estimated at ~$725bn, driving free cash flow to decade lows.  
- Anthropic pursued/closed huge funding: weighed near $1tn valuation; agreed terms of $30bn at a $900bn valuation.  
- Google + Blackstone planning a ~$5bn AI cloud venture; Google launched new Gemini AI agent.  
- Anthropic hiring high‑profile talent (Andrejj Karpathy) and briefing regulators on cyber flaws (Mythos).  
- Big Four and major firms ramping AI hiring; McKinsey changed partner pay amid AI shifts.

4) Energy transition and AI compute demand are catalyzing mega M&A in utilities and power infrastructure.  
- NextEra agreed to buy Dominion (deal cited at $66.8–$67bn) to create a ~$400–420bn US utility group.  
- FT flagged a $420bn “Project Astra” megamerger framing power build‑out for AI demand.

5) Geopolitical realignment and defence pressures are increasing geopolitical risk premia and industrial mobilization.  
- Trump‑Xi summit produced a “board of trade” and trade/taiwan tensions remain central.  
- Trump paused a planned strike on Iran and signalled possible nuclear‑deal talks; US extended some Russian oil waivers.  
- Russia and China signalled mutual backing; Putin headed to Beijing to discuss major gas projects.  
- NATO pressing European arms makers to boost investment and production; multiple defence deals and procurement moves reported.

6) Market plumbing and capital‑markets dynamics show stress: private credit, fundraising and IPOs are focal points.  
- Blue Owl retail fundraising evaporated amid private credit concerns; Astorg fundraising struggling.  
- SpaceX IPO preparation could be the largest ever; one hedge fund may receive a ~$20bn stake.  
- Polymarket launched private‑company prediction markets (SpaceX, Anthropic); investor equity allocations rose to record in May despite bond weakness.

7) Corporates are accelerating cost cuts and labour shifts as AI adoption and external shocks compress margins and disrupt supply.  
- Standard Chartered / StanChart announced plans to cut ~7,000–8,000 roles.  
- Meta and other tech firms initiating layoffs; banks and auditors posting more AI roles than traditional hires.  
- Samsung union talks extended to avert a strike that could threaten global supply chains.  
- UK job vacancies tumbled to a five‑year low; emerging market currencies (e.g., rupee) hit record lows amid oil and yield pressures.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):  
We’re in a higher‑for‑longer rates regime driven by an energy‑led inflation shock and sustained bond repricing, while equity markets remain bifurcated — buoyed by an AI capex boom and big‑tech funding flows but vulnerable to a correction as geopolitical risk and tightening financial conditions bite. Risk premia across commodities, yields and defence sectors are elevated; liquidity and private‑credit stress are key watch points.
