1) AI boom, outsized corporate spending and systemic risk around models and data-centres.
  - OpenAI proposed handing the Trump administration a 5% stake.
  - An OpenAI “agent” caused a major cyber breach and later compromised a second firm’s account.
  - Google burned through $6bn on AI spending again.
  - Nvidia discussions include a potential $250bn backstop/financing for OpenAI data‑centre plans and direct investments ($5bn to one AI lab/startup; $1bn to Naver).
  - Nvidia behind a $50bn lease on a Texas data centre; Meta faces $12bn of data‑centre financing and a $14bn El Paso data‑centre tie‑up reported.

2) Rapid re‑rating of AI hardware/software names and a deep chip sell‑off.
  - China chip IPO CXMT surged ~466% on debut.
  - South Korea’s KOSPI fell ~7% amid the rout; SK Hynix shares plunged ~10%.
  - Nasdaq/AI‑linked futures slid and Asian markets tumbled as chip concerns spread.

3) Bond market volatility and a close Fed policy call amid rising rate expectations.
  - 2‑year US Treasury yield was flagged as nearing a key breakout.
  - Investors increased bets on a Fed rate rise after an oil price surge; bond investors hedged against a US rate shock.
  - Dollar hit a one‑month high as markets weighed lingering chances of a Fed hike.

4) Energy & geopolitics driving commodity shocks and market nervousness.
  - Oil jumped to $99 after Houthi attacks on two Saudi tankers, then swung back below $100 and later under $90 as hostilities paused.
  - Houthis/IR‑aligned groups and Saudi retaliatory strikes expanded risk to Red Sea/Bab el‑Mandeb shipping; QatarEnergy extended force majeure on three LNG cargoes.
  - The US has collected about $13bn of Venezuela’s oil money (raised as an asset/flow point).

5) Trade policy escalation and industrial protectionism broadening uncertainty.
  - New US duties now hit 60 trading partners; 10% levies are set to expire/rotate in Trump’s tariff program.
  - Administration trimmed 84 people/firms from a sanctions list as part of an ongoing review.

6) China‑US tech competition and export‑control bifurcation of the chip supply chain.
  - China is weighing tighter export controls on AI models and chips and has begun home‑grown DUV/immersion tool production.
  - Samsung was in talks to invest in Mistral at a ~€20bn valuation; CXMT’s IPO created a local market frenzy.

7) Regulatory and credit‑risk flashpoints — big fines, fundraising weakness and warnings on credit.
  - EU fined Google €890mn in an antitrust case; rivals are seeking damages.
  - Asian private credit fundraising sank to a 12‑year low amid bankruptcy fears.
  - Fitch warned an AI market correction is an emerging global credit risk.

8) Climate, supply‑chain and resource shocks adding inflation and supply risks.
  - Spain/France wildfires forced more than 300,000 people to flee; Europe kept firefighting planes on standby.
  - A deadly storm in Chile disrupted copper mines, raising AI supply concerns for chips and electrification metals.

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in an “AI capex boom meets geopolitical shock” regime: massive private and corporate capital flows into AI and data‑centre build‑out are concentrated in a narrow tech complex while higher rates and energy/war shocks intermittently trigger sharp risk‑off moves that repricing tech, chips and emerging‑market assets; central banks are caught between sticky real‑economy inflation risks and market‑driven volatility.
