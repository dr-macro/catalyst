1) Bond-market repricing is accelerating and forcing cross‑asset stress.  
- 10‑year Treasury yield breakouts with implied volatility surging.  
- Treasury sell‑off deepening; yields climbing to fresh highs.  
- Bond yields surging is weighing on stocks and gold (gold at a seven‑week low; gold futures down ~3%).

2) Energy supply shocks and a diesel crunch are driving inflation and policy moves.  
- Oil prices rising for consecutive sessions amid Middle East supply concerns.  
- UK diesel prices at record highs; US strategic petroleum reserve at lowest level since 1982.  
- White House weighing red‑dyed diesel tax relief; US reportedly considering diesel export ban.

3) AI boom fuels huge corporate capital flows, M&A and large buybacks while raising safety concerns.  
- Nvidia launches a record $150bn share buyback.  
- Nvidia‑backed Nscale pushed a $35bn IPO path.  
- AMD to buy an AI start‑up (World Labs / Fei‑Fei Li) for about $8–8.2bn.  
- Instinct raised $1bn; Samsung to inject $1bn into an AI infrastructure firm.  
- Anthropic files for IPO, warns of “existential risks” and seeks control via a “Founder LLC”; OpenAI shelves a model over safety concerns.

4) Geopolitical risk (Middle East, Russia–Ukraine) is elevated and amplifying market volatility.  
- Trump rejects Iran ceasefire and signals renewed bombing after midterms; US/Iran hold parallel mediations.  
- Ukraine‑Russia strikes and drone warfare intensifying (Kyiv hit; Kremlin urging payments for drone defenses).  
- Oil export disruptions and strategic routes (Hormuz) linked to tensions; oil exporters and LNG flows affected.

5) US–China tactical détente on trade and AI is emerging, but strategic frictions remain.  
- US and China agree to trim tariffs / start an AI dialogue under a $60bn low‑tariff regime.  
- China to cut tariffs on $60bn of goods, though key items (e.g., soybeans) excluded.  
- Trump and Xi to meet two more times after a summit stalemate.

6) Capital rotation into equity growth/AI amid fading appetite for debt and record foreign flows into US stocks.  
- Foreign capital flows into US equities hit record levels as demand for debt softens.  
- Investors note divergence: many S&P 500 names out of sync with broader market.  
- Private credit strains easing as withdrawals slow; wealthy borrowers tap PE holdings.

7) Defence rearmament and large government contracts are reshaping industrial demand and capex.  
- Raytheon awarded a $20.7bn AMRAAM missile contract.  
- Russia plans to raise 2027 military spending by ~27%.  
- EU approves five pan‑European defence projects; automakers seeking work from defence demand.

8) Corporate activity, buybacks and big‑ticket M&A are active beyond AI, with streaks of contested deals.  
- BASF’s €10.3bn bid for Evonik rejected.  
- Northern Star rejected a $27bn takeover proposal.  
- TotalEnergies boosts buybacks and dividends; Goldman’s hedge fund fees are surging.  
- Burford Capital could gain up to $1.4bn from an Apple patent outcome; Beckham takes a $51mn dividend.

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
We are in an AI‑led growth narrative concentrated in a handful of large tech names, supported by massive corporate capital deployment, but that optimism is colliding with a broadening bond repricing and commodity‑driven inflation risk from Middle East disruptions. Policy and geopolitics are now central market drivers—leaving central banks constrained, real yields higher, and volatility elevated across rates, energy and selective equities.
