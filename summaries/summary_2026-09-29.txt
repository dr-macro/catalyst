1. Bond-market rout and higher real yields are re-pricing risk across asset classes.
   - 10‑year Treasury yield: hit a 19‑year high.
   - 30‑year Treasury yield: highest since 2002.
   - Treasuries: deepening sell‑off / surge in implied volatility; bond markets described as “screaming.”
   - Gold: at a seven‑week low.
   - Junk bonds: worst month since 2022; repo borrowing and leverage rising.
   - Hedge funds: record share of the $30 trillion Treasury market.

2. Oil and diesel supply fears (Middle East war/friction) are driving a commodity shock that feeds into rates and inflation expectations.
   - Oil: prices rising for multiple sessions; second session gains noted and tight relationship with US yields since 1990.
   - Diesel: UK diesel prices at record highs; US White House weighing tax relief/red‑dyed diesel; reports Trump considering diesel export ban.
   - Strategic stocks: US SPR oil stocks at lowest since 1982; US to loan up to 40 million barrels from SPR.
   - Middle Eastern exports: rose to highest levels since the Iran war began; Saudi pipeline/loads restarting.

3. AI boom is triggering massive corporate capital moves, M&A and acute safety/regulatory scrutiny.
   - Nvidia: launches record $150 billion share buyback.
   - AMD: to acquire Fei‑Fei Li’s World Labs / World Labs deal ~ $8–8.2 billion.
   - Anthropic: IPO filings warn of “existential risks”; prospectus shows dependence on big‑tech partners and $518 billion (reported) buildout figures; leaders to control via a “Founder LLC.”
   - OpenAI: shelved a model release citing safety; ARR near $70 billion; reportedly seeking large funding (reports of $30bn round / $1.4tn valuation).

4. Market concentration and funding flows are skewing toward US tech/AI winners while IPOs and risk financing cool.
   - Foreign capital flows into US stocks: hit record levels as appetite for debt fades.
   - IPOs/postponements: Oura and other IPOs delayed; IPO postponements accelerating.
   - Equities: pronounced bifurcation—AI mega‑caps drawing capital while broader market leadership weakens.

5. Geopolitical risk (US‑Iran, Russia‑Ukraine, wider Middle East and hybrid attacks) is materially affecting trade, energy and defence spending.
   - US policy: Trump rejects Iran ceasefire proposals and hosts AI CEOs; mediators working separate US‑Iran talks.
   - Russia/Ukraine: Russia raises 2027 military spending by ~27%; Russian drone/strikes continue; Kyiv operations and contested actions cited.
   - Maritime/terror risks: Houthi statements on European ships; suspected plot at RAF Fairford; Red Sea and Hormuz routes under stress.

6. Central banks and fiscal/industrial policy are tightening or being forced to respond to supply‑side demand (AI buildout + energy) and higher yields.
   - Australia: cash rate raised to a 15‑year high.
   - Fed: officials (Cook, Barr) flag continued inflationary pressure from AI investment; watchdog flagged Fed security deficiencies.
   - BOE: balance sheet shrinkage has paused; Treasury and regulators signaling potential policy/tax changes.

7. Defence, energy infrastructure and corporate industrial capex are accelerating, reshaping demand for capital goods and supply chains.
   - Defence: Raytheon (RTX) awarded $20.7 billion AMRAAM contract; defence firms ramping missile output.
   - Space/industrial: SpaceX’s Starship reached orbit on first mission; SpaceX and AI subpoenas/inspections increasing scrutiny.
   - Energy projects: Shell‑led LNG Canada Phase 2 backed (~$23 billion); old supertankers’ value surging vs new builds.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in a regime of higher real yields and tighter market liquidity driven by a bond repricing, with commodity (oil/diesel) shocks from Middle East tensions amplifying inflation fears. At the same time, concentrated AI‑driven capex and corporate capital actions (buybacks, M&A) are splitting markets—strong performance and flows into AI/mega‑cap tech while credit markets, IPO activity and cyclical sectors face stress.
