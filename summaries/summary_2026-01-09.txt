  - $200 billion instructed purchase of mortgage bonds  
  - mortgage rates <6% (first time in ~3 years)  
  - raises doubts about Fannie/Freddie IPO plans

2) US labour market cooled in December, reinforcing a Fed pause and changing the 2026 rate path outlook.  
  - payrolls +50,000 (Dec)  
  - unemployment 4.4%  
  - markets repricing 2026 rate path; Fed signals longer pause

3) Geopolitical energy moves around Venezuela are pushing oil prices and creating investor flows into energy/defense.  
  - US seizes/boards multiple tankers; industry meetings with oil majors ongoing  
  - oil +~2–3% (weekly moves reported)  
  - traders/Vitol given preliminary licence to negotiate Venezuelan oil deals

4) A campaign-led push for much bigger defence spending is driving a sharp rerating of aerospace and defence names.  
  - proposed military spending target ~$1.5 trillion (by 2027) / call for ~50% increase cited  
  - defence and aerospace stocks surged on the news

5) Equity market breadth is shifting: broad YTD gains exist even as tech cools and rotation accelerates, but momentum is waning.  
  - S&P500 up YTD even with tech weakness  
  - some individual stocks +10%+ YTD  
  - commentators flag rotation from last year’s winners into beaten-down cyclicals

6) Automakers are materially marking down EV programs and retrenching capital plans after demand/strategy resets.  
  - GM: ~$7.1 billion Q4 charges (other reports cite ~$6 billion writedown)  
  - Stellantis cancels some plug‑in hybrid lines; Rivian recalls ~20,000 vehicles

7) Commodities and mining are re‑emerging as strategic themes amid consolidation talk and supply tightness.  
  - Rio Tinto / Glencore buyout talks to form a mega‑miner (~$207–260 billion reported)  
  - copper at/near record highs; warnings of large copper shortages

8) Global inflation dynamics and China’s recovery remain uneven, keeping policy uncertainty high.  
  - China consumer inflation at a near 3‑year high; PPI deflation easing  
  - UN growth forecast for 2026 ~2.7%; eurozone inflation eased to ~2% (Dec)

9) Legal and trade risks are live and headline‑sized, with potential large balance‑sheet consequences for importers.  
  - possible Supreme Court tariff ruling could expose importers to ~$150 billion in refund risk  
  - ongoing litigation and tariff policy remain market‑sensitive

Current macro/markets regime: policy‑led and geopolitically driven — fiscal and executive actions (housing bond buys, defence spending, Venezuela/oil moves) are directly moving prices while the Fed watches a softer labour print and holds policy steady. That combination favours cyclicals, defence, energy and commodities amid fading tech leadership and elevated legal/trade tail risks, with rates and bond markets repricing cautiously for a later and smaller cut cycle.
