1) Bond market rout and rising long‑end yields is forcing policy and liquidity interventions.  
   - US 30‑year yield hit a 19‑year high ~5.33% and multi‑year highs across government bonds.  
   - Japan 10‑yr yield hit a three‑decade peak; UK gilts and German borrowing costs at multi‑year highs.  
   - Treasury to double sizes of some buybacks to at least $4bn and increase long‑term debt buybacks.  
   - Fed minutes: policymakers said more hikes likely if inflation does not cool.

2) Geopolitical escalation in the Middle East and around Hormuz is lifting oil, shipping risk and risk premia.  
   - Oil back near three‑week highs; Hormuz crossings remain in single digits; vessel struck in Strait of Hormuz with crew casualty.  
   - UAE suspends trade with Iran; Iran weighs strikes on Europe; US provides $206m for Gaza peacekeeping force.  
   - Ukraine launched 620 drones at Moscow region; heavy military logistics strains (Patriot launchers running empty).

   - Samsung raised chipmaking prices up to 15% on demand spike; SK Hynix plans >40 trillion won (~$29bn) buyback/cancellation.  
   - China routes state tankers to avoid chokepoints; “Ice Silk Road” Arctic route trials.

5) AI‑related governance, security and firm‑level stress causing market shocks and big trading losses.

   - OpenAI slowed model training after a Hugging Face hack; head of ethics left within a year.  
   - OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Teens and expanded model monitoring.

6) Risk re‑pricing is compressing tech/high‑growth valuations even as pockets of speculative rallies persist.  
   - Chip and AI stocks slid with bond sell‑off; Nasdaq and major tech names corrected.  
   - Unitree (Chinese humanoid robot maker) surged c.542–600% on Shanghai debut.  
   - Some big tech/data‑centre projects face insurance, carbon and local planning constraints (lenders/insurers exposed).

7) Broader macro stress—slowing China investment, commodity shocks and credit strain—adds downside risk to growth.  
   - China investment slump deepens; China’s 10‑yr bond yield fell to a 13‑month low amid weakness.  
   - Grain prices and global shipping costs up as Ukraine war affects Black Sea exports; diesel prices surging in the US.  
   - Private credit under strain; troubled loans rising and private equity deals in China have stalled.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
Long‑end real yields and term premia have repriced materially, driven by fiscal strain and renewed inflation/war risk, provoking bouts of volatility and policy‑side interventions (Treasury buybacks, central bank vigilance). Markets are bifurcated: durable capex into AI/data‑centres and defence is fueling pockets of demand and M&A, while bond repricing, geopolitics and China weakness are re‑rating risk across equities, credit and commodities.
