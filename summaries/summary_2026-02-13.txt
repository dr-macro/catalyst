1) AI frenzy is driving huge capital flows, corporate re‑ratings and sector disruption.  
   - Anthropic: $30bn funding round; valuation reported at $350bn (FT) and $380bn (Reuters).  
   - Mistral revenues > $400mn as Europe pushes AI independence.  
   - SoftBank: $4.2bn gain from OpenAI stake.  
   - OpenAI/other AI firms: senior staff exits and product shutdowns reported; Microsoft/OpenAI ties loosening.  
   - Hyperscaler capex and data centres: Meta building $10bn Indiana data centre; electricity prices rising faster than inflation.  
   - AI panic hitting sectors: transport/trucking, Indian IT (≈$50bn market cap hit reported), commercial real estate sell‑off.

2) Markets are volatile: tech-led equity sell‑offs, flight to longer-duration bonds and safe havens after mixed macro prints.  
   - US CPI Jan: +2.4% y/y (below expectations).  
   - US jobs: +130,000 added in January.  
   - US Jan budget deficit: $95bn.  
   - Major tech sell‑offs: Amazon in bear market; long Treasuries rally; gold and silver bouncing from lows.  
   - Asian & European markets weaker following US tech rout.

3) Central banks are diverging: a Fed on pause amid strong labour data, while other global banks move independently.  
   - Fed: interest‑rate pause reinforced by jobs data (markets debating next moves).  
   - ECB: extending its longest pause since pre‑zero rates.  
   - Russia: surprise 50bp rate cut; central bank signals further easing.  
   - Bank forecasts/changes: BofA trimmed Turkey rate‑cut expectations to 50bp for March.

4) Geopolitics and trade frictions are reshaping investment and supply strategies.  
   - US‑Taiwan trade deal: tariffs cut to 15% (deal to boost US purchases).  
   - US concludes Alibaba and BYD have links to Chinese military.  
   - US military posture: largest carrier and a second carrier/200 troops deployment reports to Middle East/Nigeria.  
   - OPEC+: leaning toward resuming output increases from April; Venezuela oil sales to bring ~$5bn in months.

5) Strategic materials and energy supply chains are under pressure, prompting sourcing shifts.  
   - Rare earths: customers urgently seeking alternatives to Chinese suppliers; China briefing metals firms on export controls.  
   - Sugar: prices hit five‑year low amid weight‑loss drug impacts.  
   - Oil: prices drifting lower on supply forecasts; global demand growth seen slowing (IEA).

6) Corporate and political scandals plus regulatory moves are raising governance and policy risk.  
   - Anthropic/AI governance headlines coexist with multiple corporate departures (OpenAI, xAI, DP World, EY partners).  
   - Legal/regulatory: Goldman lawyer Kathy Ruemmler to step down after disclosures; Pam Bondi under scrutiny in hearings.  
   - Policy shifts: Trump administration revokes EPA endangerment finding; US House votes to overturn tariffs on Canada; watchdogs warn Trump policies add $1.4tn to US deficit.

7) Real‑economy cracks (housing, consumer & services) coexist with pockets of resilience.  
   - US existing home sales: down to a >2‑year low in January; Realtors report >8% fall in January home sales.  
   - Fast‑food/consumer soft spots: big names report weaker demand; luxury and some cyclicals still outperform.  
   - Corporate actions: Heineken to cut 6,000 jobs; Kraft Heinz halts break‑up plan; major M&A moves (Nuveen/Schroders ~$13.5bn / £9.9bn).

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in an AI‑led structural re‑rating cycle where massive private capital and hyperscaler capex coexist with episodic risk‑off selling; inflation is cooling (CPI ~2.4%) but labour and geopolitical shocks keep central banks cautious and policy paths divergent. That combination is producing volatile equities (tech vulnerability), rallying long-duration bonds and elevated macro‑political risk premia across commodities and strategic supply chains.
