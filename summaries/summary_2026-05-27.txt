1) Middle East kinetic flare-up is driving an energy shock and shipping risk that is tilting markets into higher inflation and safe‑haven moves.  
   - Brent crude +4% (May 25)  
   - Multiple US strikes on Iranian boats/missile sites; Iran accuses US of ceasefire violations  
   - Strait of Hormuz still disrupted; analysts warn it may remain closed for months  
   - UK household bills set for a two‑year high; Iran war could add billions to US interest costs

2) Bond markets are reasserting themselves — real yields and liquidity stress are starting to cap equity upside.  
   - Real yields near 20‑year highs (May 20)  
   - Treasury borrowing accelerates and liquidity conditions tightening (May 25)  
   - Mortgage rates hit a nine‑month high; refinance demand down ~18%  

3) Central banks are turning hawkish or keeping options open despite fragile growth, raising the bar for risk assets.  
   - ECB policymakers laying groundwork for a June rate rise  
   - BOJ warns temporary energy shock could become persistent  
   - New Zealand holds narrowly but signals hikes ahead; Sri Lanka hikes 100bp amid energy/inflation shock

4) AI and semiconductors are concentrating market leadership and corporate capital spending, but also spurring regulation and governance risks.  
   - Nvidia plans $150 billion a year investment in Taiwan (CEO comment)  
   - Micron and SK Hynix join the $1 trillion market‑cap club; chip ETF reached $10bn fastest on record  
   - OpenAI Foundation commits $250 million for AI/economic transition work; guardrails stripped from major models in minutes

5) Equity indices have reached record highs but with narrowing breadth and a vanishing equity risk premium, raising correction risk if yields continue to rise.  
   - S&P 500 and Nasdaq hit record closing highs (late May)  
   - Analysts warn the risk premium for holding stocks over bonds is vanishing  
   - Goldman lifts S&P target to 8,000 (bullish earnings outlook)

6) Geopolitical fragmentation and industrial policy are accelerating de‑risking and supply‑chain reshaping across Europe and Asia.  
   - EU stablecoin project backed by 37 banks  
   - EU plans to force some companies to source parts from non‑Chinese suppliers  
   - Samsung planning $1.5 billion chip testing plant in Vietnam; Europe presses trade crackdown on China

7) Energy/commodity and fertilizer supply strains are widening, feeding real economy stress in emerging markets and food insecurity in advanced economies.  
   - Fertilizer groups cut production amid sulphur squeeze; China allows fresh urea exports to ease tightness  
   - Pakistan and other countries boosting oil/reserve plans after Hormuz constraints  
   - New York Fed flags a “remarkable increase” in food insecurity; low‑income households under pressure

8) Strategic space, satellite and defence spending is rising, creating new procurement winners and governance/friction points with private providers.  
   - SpaceX awarded $2.29 billion Space Force contract; Starship test strengthens IPO case  
   - Ukraine arms maker launches satellites to reduce US reliance; Starlink and Amazon may bid for EU mobile satellite spectrum  
   - Pentagon disputes over Starlink pricing during the Iran war

9) Social and fiscal strains — falling birthrates, shrinking real wages and tighter consumer pockets — are becoming structural headwinds to growth.  
   - Real wages beginning to shrink in developed countries  
   - US consumers face spending squeeze as one‑off tax rebates fade; mortgage pressure and rising household bills evident  
   - Birth rates falling “everywhere all at once” (broad demographic trend)

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in a late‑cycle, geopolitically‑stressed environment where a tech‑led equity rally is increasingly dependent on a narrow set of AI/semiconductor winners while bond markets and central banks push back via higher real yields and tighter liquidity. Energy and defense shocks are raising inflation risk and fiscal costs, so market leadership is bifurcated — large cap tech and defence suppliers outperform while real‑economy indicators (wages, consumer sentiment, housing) show strain.
