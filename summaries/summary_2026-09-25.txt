1) Bond market is in a sustained selloff driving long-term yields sharply higher and repricing risk across assets.  
  - US 10‑year likely to move above and stay above 5%  
  - US long-term borrowing costs at highest levels since 2004  
  - 30‑year Treasury yields hit highest since 2004; 10‑year reached a 19‑year high  
  - Global bond selloff deepens; Treasury yields surged most since prior tariff shock

2) Fed expectations have shifted hawkish as real yields rise, prompting talk of larger hikes and tighter oversight.  
  - Rising real yields flagged as a major Fed policy shift  
  - 50 basis‑point hike explicitly discussed as a possibility  
  - Fed planning to raise bank oversight thresholds

3) Higher yields are translating into materially higher consumer borrowing costs and strain on housing affordability.  
  - US mortgage rates breached 7%; 30‑year fixed at ~7.45%  
  - Mortgage rates pushed past 7% in multiple reports

8) Market positioning is bifurcated: AI optimism attracts flows but rising yields and geopolitical risk force de‑risking and rotation.  
  - Global equity funds snapped a two‑week outflow as AI optimism returned  
  - Equities fell on surging Treasury yields; S&P/US stocks showed intraday volatility and marginal declines  
  - Traders increasing use of bond‑replacement trades and liquidity‑sensitive strategies as bond liquidation pressures small caps

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
We are in a hawkish, high‑real‑yield regime driven by stronger growth expectations and Fed tightening, producing a broad bond rout that is lifting borrowing costs, squeezing housing and corporate refinancing, and forcing rapid portfolio repricing. Overlaying that are AI‑driven, large‑scale capital deployments and concentrated credit issuance plus episodic geopolitical oil‑supply shocks — a higher‑volatility, higher‑funding‑cost environment where liquidity and credit risk are front‑of‑mind.
