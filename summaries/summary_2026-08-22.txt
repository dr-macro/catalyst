  - US government debt: $40tn  
  - Treasury/Scott Bessent targeting the $32tn Treasury market with long‑bond buybacks  
  - Treasury announced boosted long‑term bond purchases; buybacks described as small/insufficient  
  - Long‑dated yields hit multi‑year highs and treasury prices slid

2) An escalating AI price and product war is compressing margins and reshaping corporate strategy and M&A.  
  - OpenAI/Anthropic price competition; OpenAI cuts developer pricing for GPT‑5.6 Sol by >20%  
  - Anthropic IPO preparation and enterprise data‑retention policy change  
  - Stripe to buy OpenRouter in an $8bn deal; Google-Marvell $12bn AI chip deal  
  - Nvidia customers warned of >15% server price hikes; Nvidia disclosed $21bn stake in SpaceX

3) Geopolitical escalation around Iran and Gulf security is translating directly into energy-market disruption.  
  - UAE suspended trade with Iran after claiming missile strike  
  - US announced tougher measures / “economic warfare” on Iran; Iran threatens military responses including in Europe  
  - US military aided passage of ~660 million barrels through Hormuz since May (operational support cited)  
  - Iranian oil offers to Chinese buyers weakened

4) AI hardware, hyperscaler capex and data‑centre expansion are creating power, supply‑chain and carbon challenges.  
  - Google signed $12bn AI chip deal with Marvell  
  - Nvidia and others investing in data‑centre power (Cloverleaf, Nvidia talks)  
  - Reports of >15% server price hikes driven by memory costs  
  - Big tech data‑centre boom projected to raise carbon emissions

5) Energy‑and‑trade logistics shocks (diesel, grain, tankers, Panama/El Niño) are tightening supply and raising near‑term cost risk.  
  - Soaring diesel prices; FT highlighted $5 diesel in US context  
  - Grain prices surged after Ukrainian export disruption  
  - Tanker demand spiking as Gulf producers find alternate export routes  
  - Panama Canal to cap daily transits amid the strongest El Niño forecast

6) Risk assets are rotating fast: crypto and gold rally while quant/hedge strategies and niche equity themes suffer heavy losses.  
  - Bitcoin surged toward ~$80,000 (weekly jump ~22%)  
  - Gold rallied to a 3‑month high (and above $4,600 in some coverage)  
  - Short sellers made ~$2bn on collapsing modular nuclear reactor names  
  - Quant hedge funds had worst day in two years; Hudson River made $11.4bn trading windfall

7) The Ukraine war and wider military tensions are stressing allied logistics and capability inventories.  
  - Reports that Patriot launchers are running empty, leaving Ukraine exposed  
  - Russian missile/drone attacks on Kyiv; a double‑tap strike killed at least 16 in a shopping centre  
  - Western leaders to co‑chair a Ukraine meeting; France accelerating air‑defence deliveries

8) Credit and private‑markets strains are emerging amid large corporate AI debt raises and data‑security incidents.  
  - Broadcom pursuing AI‑related debt financing reported >$60bn (some reports up to ~$70bn)  
  - Private credit under strain as troubled loans swell; investors report fatigue on corporate AI debt surge  
  - Apollo and others disclosed data breaches; Guggenheim fund trading distress noted

9) Trade and political frictions are increasing policy uncertainty and prompting retaliatory measures.  
  - US accuses >40 countries of helping China avoid US tariffs  
  - US‑Canada negotiations repeatedly shifted: tariffs paused after an announced deal then talks collapsed and tariffs took effect; Canada announced retaliatory tariffs  
  - Mark Carney described Canada‑US dispute as effectively a trade war

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a late‑cycle, policy‑sensitive regime: rising long yields, active fiscal/treasury intervention and elevated geopolitical risk are driving higher volatility and safe‑haven flows, while an AI investment boom concentrates tech capital and supply‑chain stress (power, memory, chip access). Market leadership is bifurcated — big‑cap AI/energy winners alongside stressed credit/private‑market pockets and recurring liquidity shocks in the bond complex.
