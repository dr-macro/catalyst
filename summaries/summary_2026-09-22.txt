1) AI boom: rapid monetization, huge burn rates, security breaches and rising regulatory/political scrutiny are forcing markets and policymakers to react.
- Anthropic profitable for a second straight quarter
- Anthropic IPO moved to November
- Investors warn Anthropic may struggle to sustain revenues post‑IPO
- OpenAI expects to burn $280bn by 2030
- Big tech using guarantees to keep ~$300bn of AI exposure off balance sheets
- Gemini/Google hacked three companies; OpenAI breached by researchers using Anthropic models
- AI chatbots give wrong answers “most of the time”; medical AI has a “proof problem”
- US–China AI safety talks to resume (next meeting in Shenzhen in ~2 months); Trump announces an “AI Force”

2) Bond market repricing and hawkish Fed messaging are re-setting duration and funding risks across portfolios.
- US 10‑year yield likely to get above and stay above 5%
- Wall Street expects ~ $1tn of short‑term US Treasury issuance
- Treasury yields surged; rising real yields flagged as a major Fed policy shift
- Fed officials and markets signaling more rate hikes may be needed

3) Energy and commodity supply shocks are sustaining price and security volatility, with knock‑on inflation and fiscal costs.
- US Strategic Petroleum Reserve stocks fallen to their lowest since 1982
- Global diesel shortage likely to last into 2027
- Tanker shortage threatening to keep gas prices high
- Saudi East‑West pipeline shutdown; Saudi ramped up Gulf exports after an attack
- Moscow oil refinery output halted after a drone attack; Houthis launched a missile at Riyadh
- Venezuela nearing deal to tap a disputed $4bn gold reserve
- UK homeowners facing ~£840/year mortgage hit from Iran war

4) Risk appetite is concentrated in AI/tech and crypto, driving market leadership but increasing concentration risk.
- AMD joined the $1tn market‑cap club
- Bitcoin hit ~$86,000 (8‑month high)
- Nasdaq and major tech names (Meta, Nvidia) powered a fresh rally; options and AI‑related flows surged

5) Geopolitical and security frictions are intensifying around the US, China, Russia, Iran and regional actors, affecting trade, defence and energy corridors.
- Xi to visit the US on Sept 23–25
- Russia’s pro‑Putin party won ~57% of the vote and 355 parliamentary seats
- US planning two new military bases in Greenland
- Iran activated land routes to bypass US blockade; Poland to supply Ukraine LNG/fuel
- Trump publicly pressing for restraint on strikes against refineries and claiming a Russia–Ukraine energy “truce”

6) Corporate/regulatory stresses, data incidents and deal friction are increasing legal and operational tail‑risks for financials and tech.
- Revolut handed nearly 700 customers’ data to scammers
- Irish regulator fined Google ~€403m (location‑data processing)
- Paramount reached settlement to clear path for a ~$110bn Warner Bros deal
- Oracle carrying ~$18bn of data‑centre debt pressure; SoftBank launched one of the largest junk‑bond deals to fund its OpenAI bet
- Carlyle’s stalled Lukoil deal left refineries idle amid tight markets

7) Infrastructure constraints—data centres, refineries and shipping—are creating bottlenecks that amplify inflationary and operational risk.
- US data centres “short six NYCs of electricity”
- California passed broad data‑centre oversight; EU to require data‑centre energy/water disclosures; Texas ordered a data‑centre permit halt
- Oil stocks in SPR and refinery outages tightening fuel supply; tanker shortage lifting shipping rates

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a high‑rate, high‑uncertainty regime: policy tightening and rising real yields are pressuring duration while equities concentrate in AI/tech and crypto, creating asymmetric risk‑on dynamics. Simultaneous energy and supply‑chain shocks plus elevated geopolitical risk are feeding inflation and fiscal strain, keeping volatility elevated across bonds, commodities and selected credit pockets.
