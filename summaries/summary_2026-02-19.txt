1. Geopolitical risk is driving safe-haven flows and commodity volatility around Iran and the Russia–Ukraine war.  
   - Oil up ~4% on Iran‑US tensions; crude > $70/bbl.  
   - Gold surged >2% and held near $5,000/oz.  
   - Iran briefly shut Hormuz for a few hours; US military build‑up in region and Trump reportedly giving a ~10‑day window to decide on action.  
   - Russia pummels Ukraine’s power grid; Russian casualties reported to be surging; Ukraine exports (grain, iron ore) hit by strikes.  
   - Sweden pledges $1.4bn in military aid to Ukraine.

2. AI, chips and data‑centre investment are accelerating a tech re‑rating and capex boom.  
   - Nvidia struck a multibillion‑dollar chip deal with Meta.  
   - Micron planning ~$200bn to address AI memory bottleneck.  
   - Reliance to invest $110bn in AI infrastructure.  
   - Sequoia led a $1bn seed round; Fei‑Fei Li’s World Labs raised $1bn; Mistral/other AI deals and buys noted.  
   - 25‑year‑old founder raised $220mn for a UK AI‑chip start‑up; Taalas raised $169mn; Yotta/Nvidia $2bn for India AI push.  
   - Reports show AI productivity pick‑up becoming visible and >50% of enterprise software could switch to AI (CEO claim).

3. Central bank policy uncertainty: Fed minutes reveal a split, rate‑cut odds trimmed and the dollar remains volatile.  
   - Fed minutes show officials split and a reduced appetite for near‑term rate cuts; Fed “appears in no rush to cut.”  
   - Fed official Goolsbee said “several” cuts possible if inflation returns to 2%.  
   - Dollar traded near a two‑week high; fund managers have their most bearish stance on the dollar in a decade.  
   - US 10‑year Treasury yield described as trading near “fair value”; shorter‑ and longer‑dated pressures flagged in rates outlooks.

4. Bond market technicals and supply considerations are reasserting themselves, with regional central‑bank shifts adding uncertainty.  
   - Multiple “Rates Outlook” notes: long‑dated pressures rebuilding; supply dynamics described as a global story.  
   - Dutch pension funds may prepare early for 2027 transitions; EUREP expansion and euro globalisation discussed.  
   - ECB chief Christine Lagarde reported planning to leave before term end; digital euro estimated to cost EU banks €4–6bn over 4 years.

5. Commodities and industrials are benefiting from higher risk premia and real‑asset demand amid supply constraints.  
   - Gold near $5,000/oz; copper and rare earths saw price gains; rare earths rose above price floor.  
   - Oil prices jumped (one report +4%); US crude inventories posted hefty declines.  
   - Airbus flagged engine shortages holding back A320 production and lowering delivery targets; Airbus Q4 profit up 7% but 2026 deliveries seen below estimates.

6. Market leadership is bifurcating: large AI winners and data‑centre plays rally while software and some tech names sell off.  
   - Wall Street lifted by Nvidia and other AI stocks; tech stocks rose for a second straight day.  
   - Software sell‑off continued; Palo Alto shares fell after earnings; “software sell‑off part II” noted.  
   - Raspberry Pi stock jumped ~40% on AI chatter; investor rotation into infrastructure and chip makers reported.

7. Corporate and credit stress flashes: private credit and alternative funds face liquidity strains while large M&A and settlements persist.  
   - Blue Owl halted redemptions at one fund and permanently halted redemptions at a retail‑aimed private credit fund; private credit stocks slid.  
   - Bayer proposed a $7.25bn plan to settle Roundup claims.  
   - Getir founders suing Mubadala for $700mn; Hapag‑Lloyd to buy Zim for $4.2bn.

8. Trade, tariffs and structural policy remain politically charged and feeding economic frictions.  
   - New York Fed: US businesses and consumers bear ~90% of tariff costs.  
   - US trade deficit rose to $901bn in 2025 despite tariffs; imports at record highs.  
   - Domestic industrial policy moves: US/Japan mega‑projects under a $550bn deal; calls for more strategic European energy/defence policy; IMF urging China to halve industrial subsidies.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a risk‑off yet bifurcated regime: geopolitics (Iran, Russia–Ukraine) is elevating commodity and safe‑haven premia while AI‑led capex and chip optimism concentrate equity gains in a narrow group. Central banks remain cautious and divided — slowing expected cuts keeps bond yields and the dollar volatile — and liquidity stresses in private credit add a second‑order financial‑stability risk.
