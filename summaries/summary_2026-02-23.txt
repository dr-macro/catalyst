1) Trade policy chaos is re‑shaping risk and supply chains as a US tariff regime is legally upended and re‑issued by the administration.  
- Supreme Court struck down Trump’s sweeping global tariffs  
- Administration first imposed a 10% replacement, then raised it to 15%  
- Illinois governor invoiced $8.6bn for tariff refunds  
- US to stop collecting tariffs deemed illegal; EU/other partners delay/reevaluate trade deals

2) Geopolitical risk around Iran and the Middle East is spiking, prompting a large US military buildup and market sensitivity to conflict risk.  
- US gathered the most air power in the Middle East since the 2003 Iraq invasion  
- President gave Iran a 15‑day deadline for a deal; talks slated (Geneva/Oman) while strikes remain possible  
- Satellite imagery and reporting show a surge in US fighter jets and military hardware in the region

3) An AI capex boom is underway but is simultaneously driving disruption, regulatory friction and corporate stress across tech and defence.  
- OpenAI expects roughly $600bn of compute spend through 2030  
- Micron plans ~$200bn investment to address AI memory bottlenecks  
- Nvidia/OpenAI scaled an unfinished $100bn deal down in favour of a $30bn investment  
- Big tech/bridgewater reporting ~ $650bn of AI investment in 2026; Amazon plans $12bn in Louisiana data centres  
- Data centres seek credit ratings to unlock funding; Perplexity drops ads; Anthropic faces Pentagon friction and IP concerns

4) Markets are oscillating between safe‑haven flows and risk‑off rotation as macro data disappoints and uncertainty rises.  
- US Q4 GDP growth slowed to a 1.4% annual rate; inflation stays sticky (~3%)  
- Gold jumped (headlines cite gold above $5,100) while the dollar has softened  
- US 10‑year Treasury yield trading near ~4.07% in recent reads; yields and Treasury bid/ask dynamics volatile  
- U.S. 30‑year mortgage rate dipped below 6%

5) Private credit and parts of the financial sector are showing acute liquidity and valuation stress that could amplify market dislocations.  
- Blue Owl permanently halted redemptions at a retail‑facing private credit fund  
- Private credit stocks slid; bids/offers for private funds and secondary stakes surfaced (Saba/others)  
- Coverage flags a private equity/PE exit logjam and record difficulty selling positions

6) Sector rotation and headline‑driven corporate moves are intensifying volatility — AI fears hammer software and cyber names while selective M&A and trial results move healthcare and consumer names.  
- Software and private‑capital shares saw heavy selling (reports of >$200bn market‑cap swings cited)  
- IBM fell ~11% on Anthropic‑related fears; cybersecurity stocks dropped on AI‑threat headlines  
- Novo Nordisk shares plunged (~10%) after an obesity drug failed to beat a rival; Klarna stock fell 27% after loan‑loss hit  
- Gilead to acquire Arcellx for up to $7.8bn; Volvo to recall ~40,000 EVs

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in a higher‑uncertainty, multi‑shock regime: policy and geopolitical shocks (tariff/legal turmoil, Iran tensions) are driving flight‑to‑safety flows while an AI‑driven capex boom fuels concentrated tech investment and sectoral disruption. Real growth is softening (US Q4 GDP ~1.4%), inflation remains sticky, and rates/credit markets are volatile as investors reprice risk and liquidity in private markets.
