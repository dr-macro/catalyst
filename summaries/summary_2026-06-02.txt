1) AI-fuelled capital boom is re-shaping markets, corporate spending and IPO supply.  
- Alphabet plans to raise $80bn via stock sales  
- Anthropic finalised $65bn funding and filed/confidential IPO paperwork; expanding Mythos access to >15 countries and ~200 Glasswing partners  
- SpaceX/OpenAI/Anthropic positioned for “fast entry” IPOs; SpaceX targeting ~$1.75tn valuation, set aside up to 5% for select buyers and waived lock-up  
- SoftBank pledges €75bn for a French AI facility; Kirkland & Ellis to spend $500mn on AI tech; IBM/Red Hat $5bn security pledge  
- HPE shares up ~30–37% after blowout AI-driven results; convertible bond issuance to set a record year

2) Market froth and concentration: AI/semis driving historic gains and raising bubble warnings.  
- S&P 500 on one of its fastest multi-month rises; longest winning streak in a year reported  
- Investors piling into bullish options and equity supply/IPO concerns flagged  
- Commentary from major banks: “more greed than fear” on Wall Street

3) Bond market and rates stress: real yields and liquidity strains are rising amid accelerating issuance.  
- Real yields near 20‑year highs  
- Warnings that surging bond yields threaten the stock rally  
- Liquidity tightening as Treasury borrowing accelerates; guidance on reading auctions emphasized  
- Multiple “rates outlook” pieces warn of further upside risk

4) Energy shock — Middle East conflict and Hormuz disruptions are lifting oil, fuels and commodity risk premia.  
- Oil closed up >4% after breakdowns in US‑Iran talks; US crude exports hit a record high in May  
- ADNOC flags August as a possible tipping point for prices; IEA warns risk of critically low stockpiles before peak summer demand  
- Venezuela exports ~1.25m bpd in May; OPEC+ likely to raise July output target despite Strait of Hormuz disruption  
- Aluminium at four‑year highs; refined fuel margins expected elevated through 2026

5) Geopolitical escalation is a core market driver, amplifying volatility and prompting policy moves.  
- US and Iran launched fresh strikes; US reportedly struck Iranian drone command sites  
- Israel strikes in Lebanon/Hezbollah; partial ceasefires discussed but instability persists  
- US in talks to expand nuclear deployments in Europe; US sanctions Iran’s largest crypto exchange; nine countries exited a Ukraine ammunition coalition

6) Chip/tech supply-chain and export-control battlegrounds are accelerating reshoring and capacity expansion.  
- South Korea export growth at a four‑decade high on AI chip demand  
- US moved to halt some Nvidia chip shipments to Chinese firms’ overseas units; Nvidia warns of supply constraints but launches PC “superchip”  
- SK Hynix plans to double wafer capacity over five years; Arm CEO warns a US ban on AI CPU chip exports would be hard to enforce  
- China tightens curbs on outbound deals and tech transfer

7) Inflationary squeeze on households: real wages and everyday budgets are under pressure despite strong headline labour metrics.  
- Real wages beginning to shrink in developed countries  
- US credit‑card balances ~ $1.25tn; job openings rose to ~7.6m while hiring shows weakness  
- Eurozone inflation 3.2% in May; firms struggle to pass on costs

8) Safe‑haven flows and reserve adjustments: precious metals and policy shifts reflect diminished faith in Treasuries as the only reserve anchor.  
- ECB: gold replaces US Treasuries as the world’s top reserve asset (in relative terms)  
- Gold gains on easing Treasury yields and Middle East uncertainty; Treasury yields have been volatile, falling on ceasefire hopes

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in an AI-driven equity expansion concentrated in a narrow set of big-tech and semiconductors, financed by massive capital raises and an active IPO pipeline, occurring alongside sticky inflation, rising real yields and tightening liquidity from heavy sovereign issuance. Geopolitical shocks (Middle East, Russia‑Ukraine) are feeding an energy/commodity shock that elevates inflation risk and safe‑haven flows, making the cycle vulnerable to a rates-driven market repricing.
