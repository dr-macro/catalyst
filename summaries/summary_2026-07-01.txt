   - $200bn: record US M&A boom in power sector linked to AI
   - AWS commits $1bn to embedded AI engineers
   - White House lifts ban / export controls cleared for Anthropic models (Fable/Mythos 5)
   - Five Eyes: AI-powered threats could succeed “within months”
   - BIS warning of AI “exuberance” risking a lengthy investment bust
   - Firms capping external model use and cutting AI token spend; heavy AI spenders add staff faster

2) Geopolitical war and defence rearmament reshaping budgets and supply chains: Ukraine pressures Russia, Europe and US boost arms and defense tech investment amid rising battlefield effectiveness of drones.
   - $12bn: venture capital rush into defence tech
   - €6.6bn: Ukraine seeks EU peace‑fund aid for military support
   - $2.54bn: Saab Gripen fighter jet deal for Ukraine
   - 195,000: US defence jobs sustained by Europe’s rearmament drive
   - Reports of Ukraine striking Russian facilities and Russian leadership under strain

3) Middle East friction + Hormuz disruption = volatile energy flows and quick swings in oil prices and shipping risk.
   - Oil back at “prewar” levels as Gulf flows pick up, but talks later break down and oil spikes again
   - Biggest monthly / quarterly oil declines since 2020 noted alongside fresh upside on renewed tensions
   - Mines and attacks expected to hold back Strait of Hormuz shipping for months
   - Iran reportedly selling oil at ~20% premium as exports resume

4) Market bifurcation: AI/tech-led equity gains coexist with rising yields, currency stress and a defensive repositioning into defense, small-caps and energy.
   - $2.2tn: Magnificent Seven lost that much in a recent tech rotation
   - Chip rally added ~$2tn combined value to Micron, Intel and AMD in Q2
   - S&P/Nasdaq posted best quarter since 2020 while small-caps enjoyed a strong H1
   - Gold set for worst quarterly loss in 13 years; Brent posted steep quarterly decline
   - Yen at 40-year low; WSJ dollar index +0.85% for the quarter

5) Bonds and central banks: real yields and hawkish pricing are doing the heavy lifting while Fed communication shifts to a “say less” era under new leadership.
   - Real yields >2% flagged as “doing the Fed’s job”
   - Multiple “Rates Outlook” pieces noting repricing risk at the front end and long-end anchoring by hawkish sentiment
   - New Fed chair spotlight: vows on Fed independence and signals to talk less; markets sensitive to tone

6) Industrial policy, supply-chain sovereignty and corporate cost pressure: states push for tech/defense onshoring while companies face AI cost shocks and cutbacks.
   - EU joins US pact to reduce reliance on Chinese AI supply chains; EU sets October deadline to cut China trade deficit
   - Samsung / SK Hynix mega chip investments spotlighted
   - Apple raises MacBook and iPad prices by ~20%
   - Volkswagen to cut up to 100,000 jobs; British American Tobacco cuts 9,000 jobs

7) Idiosyncratic sovereign and corporate stress alongside big deals: large restructurings and mega‑M&A continue amid regulatory frictions.
   - $240bn: Venezuela to reveal debt pile in largest restructuring
   - $2.8tn: record H1 dealmaking driven by mega takeovers
   - $111bn / $81bn: large Paramount‑Warner remediation and takeover figures under review
   - SpaceX shares plunged ~$400bn in market value during a post‑IPO reversal

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a risk-on yet fragile regime: AI-driven equity leadership and heavy M&A/IPO flows coexist with rising real yields, a strong dollar and fractured FX (yen weakness) that keep markets sensitive to central-bank tone. Elevated geopolitical risk (Ukraine, Middle East/Hormuz) is tilting capital toward defence and energy, raising volatility in commodities and credit while policymakers push industrial‑sovereignty measures.
