1) Central banks are tightening policy and bond-market rates are proving sticky.  
- ECB raised interest rates on 11 June (first move since 2023)  
- Four developed-market central banks are now hiking  
- Bank of Japan set to lift rates to a 31‑year high (large ~1% move signalled)  
- Multiple bond headlines: “sticky” rates, “bracing for a hawkish ECB”, Treasury borrowing accelerating, liquidity beginning to tighten

2) Middle East hostilities are driving energy-market volatility and feeding higher inflation.  
- US and Iran exchanged strikes; Iran announced closure of the Strait of Hormuz  
- Oil fell below $90/barrel (sank to ~$88 on peace hopes) after strikes were called off, but earlier energy shock pushed prices higher  
- US inflation jumped to 4.2% in May amid the Middle East energy shock

3) A Wall Street tech/AI and space frenzy is re-allocating capital into data‑centres, AI infra and one mega IPO.  
- SpaceX raised $75bn in the largest IPO ever (priced $135; retail orders >$70bn; market cap topped ~$2tn; shares +28% on debut)  
- SpaceX signed a $30bn deal to lease computing capacity to Google; Goldman projects SpaceX AI revenue could rise ~100x by 2030  
- Major data‑centre and AI plays: Oracle planning ~$70bn data‑centre buildout; KKR launched a $10bn AI infrastructure vehicle; widespread corporate AI spending and M&A activity (OpenAI/Anthropic price-war signals)

4) Reserve flows and safe‑haven positioning are shifting even as gold remains volatile.  
- ECB said gold has replaced US Treasuries as the world’s top reserve asset  
- Gold hit a 6‑month low and Comex gold settled down ~3.56% at $4,108.20 (other prints near $4,090 also reported); gold set for a weekly loss amid rate fears

5) Markets are swinging between risk‑on and risk‑off as growth, inflation and political shocks collide.  
- New Middle East clashes + inflation fears produced the Dow’s worst day of 2026 (headline move) and sharp intraday volatility across tech and chips  
- Stocks rallied when strikes were cancelled; space and AI names led big flows into thematic trades (and then some cooling)  
- Hedge funds reduced broader tech positions ahead of the SpaceX IPO; wholesale prices rose 1.1% in May and producer inflation posted its largest annual gain in ~3½ years

6) Growth prospects and credit/liquidity stress are rising on war spillovers, higher rates and large issuance.  
- World Bank cut global growth to 2.5% for 2026 and warned it could drop to 1.3% if war spillovers hit markets  
- Indonesia’s Danantara unit raised $1.5bn in a debut dollar bond with strong demand; AES priced $1bn senior notes; Ares raised $8.5bn for a new fund  
- Private credit/LP strains noted (examples: large private-credit funds honoring a minority of redemption requests)

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We’re in a higher‑for‑longer rate regime with sticky inflation that is increasingly energy‑sensitive, producing wide dispersion between tech/AI/space growth bids and caution in fixed income and credit; geopolitical tail risks (Middle East) are the principal volatility trigger while liquidity is tightening as public borrowing and private‑market strains rise.
