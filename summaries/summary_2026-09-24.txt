1) Bond rout and a sustained jump in government yields is reshaping risk premia across markets.  
   - US 10-year Treasury above 5% / highest since 2007  
   - US 30-year Treasury yield at highest since 2004  
   - Japan 10-year bond yield at a 30-year high  
   - Mortgage rates breach 7%  
   - Investors demanded highest yield at a two‑year Treasury auction since 2024

2) Monetary policy expectations have shifted tighter, forcing markets to price more Fed action.  
   - Rising real yields described as signalling a major Fed policy shift  
   - Fed officials (Williams) say another rate hike this year is “reasonable”  
   - Market pricing: next Fed hike expected in October (following Fed comments)

3) Heavy issuance and funding demands are amplifying term‑structure stress.  
   - Wall Street expects the US to issue about $1tn of short‑term debt as borrowing costs climb  
   - SoftBank raising $11.1bn in the world’s biggest high‑yield corporate bond sale  
   - Anthropic signs an $11.6bn cloud deal (warrant up to 5% stake)  
   - Big tech using guarantees to keep ~$300bn of AI exposure off balance sheets

4) Energy shocks and Middle East geopolitics are a primary driver of inflation and market nerves.  
   - Oil holding above $100/bbl; tanker costs hit record $1.2mn/day  
   - Diesel surge costing European drivers €203mn per day  
   - Reports of US preparing a 90‑day diesel export ban; administration denies/adjusts messaging  
   - Houthis and Iran-linked strikes escalate regional supply risk; US proposes $10bn fund to bypass Hormuz

5) Massive, capital‑intensive AI build‑out is fueling corporate financing and systemic‑risk concerns.  
   - OpenAI expects to “burn” $280bn by 2030  
   - Anthropic/OpenAI price war: cheaper models released; AI firms urged to hold models for external testing  
   - Analysts warn AI buildout raises systemic risks; insurers report ~ $1bn extra AI‑related costs

6) Equities and credit are feeling the spillovers: narrower market breadth, sectoral winners and losers.  
   - Nasdaq‑100 hit new highs on AI FOMO even as broad markets sell off  
   - Bond liquidation cited as wrecking small caps; stock futures and US equities fall as yields surge  
   - Global bond sell‑off pushes 30‑yr US yield to multi‑year highs; gold slips as Fed bets rise

7) Geopolitical diplomacy and trade moves are producing temporary truces but longer‑term fragmentation risks persist.  
   - US‑China trade truce extended to January at Trump‑Xi summit  
   - Ongoing Iran diplomacy talks alongside sanctions and flight restrictions; US invites Putin to G20 (possible)  
   - Saudi Arabia quits a China‑led cross‑border currency platform; EU lifts sanctions on two Russian oligarchs

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):  
We are in a high‑yield, high‑volatility regime driven by tight global monetary policy expectations, energy‑supply shocks and accelerated corporate capital spending on AI — all against a backdrop of elevated geopolitical risk. The result: a durable re‑pricing of rates and credit, stronger dollar pressure, pressured real assets (mortgages, small caps) and a bifurcated market where AI winners and energy‑sensitive sectors diverge.
