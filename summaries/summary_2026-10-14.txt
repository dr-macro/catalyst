1) Energy and shipping risks are driving a fresh supply shock and diesel squeeze.
   - Ships’ captains paid $100,000 a month to transit the Strait of Hormuz.
   - Two cargo ships sank / container ship hit or sank near Hormuz; 11 sailors reported missing in a related attack.
   - Saudi Aramco HQ and other oil facilities hit; US hit a commercial tanker in the Gulf of Oman.
   - IEA warns diesel shortages could persist into 2027; global diesel prices set to rise again.
   - Trump-Putin diesel deal criticized as covering less than two weeks of U.S. demand.

2) The AI boom is encountering financial, legal and regulatory pushback that is curbing leveraged expansion.
   - OpenAI annualised revenues reported $20bn lower than previously signalled.
   - Anthropic Q3 revenue reported $16.8bn; Anthropic warns models may self‑replicate.
   - Insurance and liability questions raised for Altman/Amodei over ‘rogue’ AI agents.
   - AI borrowing is slowing amid investor wariness; CoreWeave and other AI/infrastructure names plunged and their bonds/tokens faced stress (market action highlighted by large selloffs and rising bond yields on AI debt).

3) Bond markets are rapidly repricing for higher-for-longer policy, lifting real yields and the 10‑year rate outlook.
   - Analysis points to rising real yields and a potential 6% 10‑year Treasury rate.
   - Commentary and market moves discuss 10‑year yields at/around 5% as a reference entry level.
   - U.S. September inflation jumped to 3.7%, fuelling rate‑hike bets and auction watching.

4) Tech and semiconductor sectors face sharp volatility amid AI fatigue and supply constraints.
   - Chip stocks slid after executive comments (Broadcom ‘casino’ remark) and memory names fell (Micron, others).
   - ASML says AI demand will exceed chip‑tool capacity through 2028 and reports order interest into 2030.
   - China races to build data centres and AI‑related exports surged 36% in September, intensifying supply‑chain/competition dynamics.

5) Corporate and financial plumbing show stress and concentration: big asset inflows but headline fines and funding caution.
   - BlackRock pulled in $205bn of client funds, taking assets to record highs.
   - Bank of America fined $1.2bn and reported a 17% profit drop after the charge.
   - General Atlantic plans an early‑2027 IPO at up to a $10bn valuation; SoftBank seeking $100bn from Gulf investors for AI expansion.

6) Political and fiscal risks are intensifying globally, complicating policy and market outlooks.
   - IMF warns of the biggest budget deficits among top economies since Covid and calls for urgent fiscal consolidation.
   - Germany issued an EU budget ultimatum; EU debates new taxes and safeguards (e.g., for hybrid cars).
   - Geopolitical strain: NATO/European defence warnings about Russian hybrid/spy campaigns; US domestic politics and the Trump administration’s Russia diesel pact are creating allied tensions.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
Higher real yields and hotter inflation prints have shifted the regime to “higher for longer” rates, forcing a risk‑reprice: equities are volatile (AI and growth cyclicals hit hardest), credit for capital‑intensive AI/data centre plays is under scrutiny, and energy/geopolitical shocks are creating upside inflation and commodity‑price risk while fiscal strains increase tail risk for sovereigns.
