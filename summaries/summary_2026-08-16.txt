1) AI and data‑center boom is driving a frothy tech funding and IPO cycle, concentrated risk and heavy financing needs.
  - Anthropic: Q2 revenue reportedly > $11.5bn; IPO valuation models tied to $190–200bn 2028 revenue forecast; investors eyeing up to $2tn valuation.
  - Nvidia / ecosystem: disclosed $21bn stake in SpaceX; scaled back a reported $250bn OpenAI data‑center guarantee; Goldman in talks on Nvidia financing; Nvidia eyes $3bn SB Energy investment.
  - Credit stress signal: “hyperscaler” borrowing binge shaking up foreign credit markets; AI-driven surge in bond yields cited as next market risk.

2) Bond markets and Treasury issuance show rising yields, wider term premia and fragile price discovery.
  - US sold 30‑year bonds at highest borrowing costs since 2001.
  - 10‑year yield premium has risen on inflation risk and Fed uncertainty.
  - Reports flag AI‑related issuance as a driver of a potential surge in bond yields.

3) Middle East escalation (Iran / Hormuz) is a near‑term risk premium on energy, shipping and defence deployments.
  - US says it can keep a naval blockade on Iran “indefinitely”; sent additional carrier to the region; USS Abraham Lincoln deployment noted.
  - UAE/ADNOC vessel reported attacked; oil climbed > $1 after tanker attacks and was set for weekly gains.
  - Hormuz traffic slowed; mortgage and borrowing costs noted to be affected by the stand‑off.

4) FX volatility and yen intervention stress cross‑border policy coordination.
  - Historic US‑Japan intervention failed to halt the yen’s slide.
  - US euro sale to prop up the yen reportedly blindsided the ECB.
  - Commentary and traders flag a renewed, contested fight over the yen.

5) Climate extremes and resource stress are amplifying supply‑chain, insurance and agricultural risks.
  - Wildfires: record levels across Britain; Belgium’s biggest wildfire; evacuations (Germany ~2,000 people).
  - Drought / crop impact: Puerto Rico rationing water; French drought could halve maize crop; Rhine river at record low disrupting transport.
  - Heatwaves and storms driving mounting business losses and insurance gaps.

6) China is expanding strategic trade and tech reach while Western policymakers press for decoupling.
  - New Arctic “Ice Silk Road” cargo route opened.
  - China tightening grip on Europe’s car supply chain.
  - US accuses >40 countries of helping China avoid tariffs; Deutsche became first European clearing bank for renminbi.

7) Market concentration and big‑tech repositioning: heavy flows into a narrow set of winners and selective institutional repositioning.
  - Berkshire added ~$17bn to Alphabet stake; Alphabet became one of Berkshire’s largest holdings.
  - S&P 500 logged multiple weekly gains even as institutional filings show cautious positioning.
  - Large single‑firm losses and rescues: Jane Street reported ≈ $15bn hit; a US mortgage firm received a ~$2bn lifeline.

8) Policy and financial‑stability tradeoffs: central banks’ backstops and political decisions are encouraging leverage while complicating future crisis response.
  - Commentary flags central‑bank market backstops as fuelling leverage and future crises.
  - Asset managers report continued outflows and fee pressure (e.g., T. Rowe expects years to stem outflows).
  - Regulators stepping up scrutiny of prediction markets and privately‑held credit exposures.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
We are in a narrow‑leadership, risk‑on market driven by an AI and data‑centre investment boom that is concentrating returns while funding needs push longer‑term yields higher; geopolitical (Iran/Hormuz) and climate shocks are keeping energy and insurance premia elevated. Central banks remain in a delicate hold/monitor stance—supporting markets but allowing higher sovereign yields—leaving liquidity / credit fragilities and FX policy friction as the primary systemic risks.
