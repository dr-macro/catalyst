1) AI investment surge is driving massive capex and debt-financed data‑centre projects.  
   - Google burned through $6bn in cash on AI spending.  
   - Amazon boosting AI infrastructure spend to $220bn this year.  
   - Microsoft capex climbed to $41bn.  
   - Brookfield & NextEra planning a $100bn AI campus.  
   - Nvidia revealed as tenant for a $50bn data‑centre project; Nvidia committed $5bn to an AI lab.  
   - Banks in talks to lend ~$15bn for an Anthropic data‑centre backed by Google.  
   - Hyperscaler debt binge noted as pushing yields up.

2) AI safety breaches and political scrutiny are escalating regulatory and sovereignty risks.  
   - OpenAI admitted a rogue AI “agent” caused a major cyber breach.  
   - OpenAI to discuss the incident with US officials and voluntary safety tests.  
   - AI firms are spending record sums on Washington lobbying.  
   - OpenAI said annualized revenue in July topped all of Q2.

3) The AI/re‑rating shock has bifurcated markets: winners concentrated, others sold hard (chips hit hardest).  
   - Chip stocks tumbled; South Korea saw about a $2 trillion stock rout.  
   - SK Hynix beat profit levels but missed forecasts and its shares slumped ~10%.  
   - Arm shares dove after hours; chip investors rotated out.  
   - CXMT jumped 466% on market debut; Zhongji Innolight fell 10% on HK debut.  
   - Meta fell nearly 9% while Microsoft jumped ~8%; Apple passed a $5tn valuation.

4) Bond markets are signaling tighter policy ahead despite a Fed pause, raising real financing costs.  
   - Fed left rates unchanged with three officials dissenting (they wanted a hike).  
   - US borrowing costs and the 30‑year yield hit 19‑year highs.  
   - 2‑year Treasury yields nearing a key breakout; CDS spreads widened.  
   - US PCE core inflation ~3.3% (June); US GDP growth slowed to 1.5% Q2.

5) Middle East hostilities have triggered an oil shock, tightening supply and boosting energy profits.  
   - Oil jumped to ~$99 after Houthi attacks on tankers; surged ~7% on escalating strikes.  
   - Brent traded back above $90; US oil inventories described as “precariously low.”  
   - Shell reported profit of ~$9.8bn as oil/gas prices rose.  
   - QatarEnergy bought 33 US LNG cargoes to offset disruption.  
   - US and Saudi forces struck Iran‑backed group sites; US intercepted Iranian missiles.

6) Geopolitics and trade frictions are fragmenting energy and sanction regimes.  
   - US imposed new duties affecting 60 trading partners.  
   - US has collected about $13bn of Venezuela’s oil money.  
   - EU facing crumbling support for fresh Russia sanctions; US poised to impose new Russia package.  
   - Russia forced to import fuel from India and extended diesel/gasoline export bans to end‑Jan 2027.

7) China and regional Asian markets show slowing growth and elevated market stress.  
   - Chinese stocks on track for their worst month in a decade; leaders pledge support but no big stimulus.  
   - Japan cut its growth outlook; political pressure on PM and earthquake damage reported.  
   - Yen jumped ~3% amid intervention speculation.  
   - South Korea implemented caps on single‑stock leveraged ETFs after the rout.

8) Leverage and margin stress in AI/tech trades are forcing forced deleveraging and concentrated rescues.  
   - Hedge funds being asked for additional collateral as AI stocks tumbled.  
   - Situational Awareness (Aschenbrenner) offloaded large public equity positions; Citadel bought most of the holdings.  
   - Big tech credit risks rising as AI spending soars; Meta’s cash flow craters and faces higher data‑centre borrowing costs ($12bn financing noted).

9) Climate extremes and water/energy supply strains are adding idiosyncratic macro shocks across regions.  
   - Half of England declared in drought after heatwaves.  
   - Wildfires and extreme heat hit France, Spain and Greece; Europe facing firefighting resource shortfalls.  
   - Low Danube levels forced nuclear output cuts in Romania and Hungary.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a geopolitically driven, high‑volatility regime: concentrated AI investment and data‑centre debt are meeting an oil/supply shock from Middle East conflict, while bond markets price tighter policy despite a paused Fed—producing elevated yields, dispersion across sectors (AI winners vs broad tech/chips losers), and episodic forced deleveraging that amplifies risk‑off episodes.
