1) AI/data-centre backlash triggering a sharp tech sell‑off and repricing of the AI growth trade.  
  - Nasdaq: ~$2.5tn wiped (Reuters) / Wall Street shed $3.2tn (FT)  
  - Nasdaq entered correction; biggest daily drop in 19 months; Magnificent 7 lost ~$1tn (MarketWatch/WSJ)  
  - South Korea stocks -8%; Nikkei -6% (Reuters/WSJ)  
  - Anthropic IPO targeting $1.5tn; IPO timing/valuation odds slipping (FT/Reuters)  
  - Protests, local curbs and political pushback against data centres (activists, governors, state laws reported)

2) Democratic midterm “blue wave” reshaped policy risk and accelerated market repositioning away from unchecked tech expansion.  
  - Democrats swept midterms; control of Congress leading to planned AI bills and probes (Reuters/FT)  
  - US stocks posted best day since August after Democrats sweep (FT)  
  - Tech donors/CEOs saw large paper losses (e.g., Musk ≈$120bn hit reported)

3) Central banks and inflation dynamics remain hawkish, keeping rate‑sensitive assets under pressure.  
  - Bank of England hiked to 4% (first rise since 2023) and signalled more may come (Reuters/FT)  
  - Eurozone inflation 4% in October (FT) and UK survey expects inflation to peak 4.4% in 2027 (FT)  
  - US jobs: +165,000 in October; unemployment 4.1% — Fed seen likely to hold to a December hike (FT/Reuters/WSJ)

4) Bond markets and UK fiscal risk became a focal point ahead of new budgets, amplifying gilt market volatility.  
  - Chancellor Healey’s £15bn fiscal buffer cited as vulnerable to gilt-market moves (Healey headlines)  
  - Multiple pieces on tests the gilt market will impose on Healey’s first budget and warning that bond market may “take matters into its own hands”

5) Credit stress concentrated in data‑centre and related financing, with cracks in specialised high‑yield paper and bank exposures.  
  - ING reportedly marketing $500m of data‑centre loans for sale (Reuters)  
  - CoreWeave bond yield topped 16% (CNBC)  
  - Credit spreads: “calm on surface, concern at the bottom” and hedge funds cutting risk / handing back capital (Investing.com/FT/WSJ)

6) Energy and commodity volatility driven by Mideast supply risks and shifting demand expectations after the vote.  
  - Oil tumbled below $100 on post‑election peace hopes, then Brent climbed back above $100 as supply risks persisted (FT/WSJ)  
  - US diesel supplies at 20‑year low; refinery attacks and tanker hits reported (FT/Reuters)  
  - Qatar set to become world’s largest buyer of US LNG (FT)

7) Safe‑haven flows and market volatility spiked as growth/inflation tradeoffs and geopolitics converged.  
  - Dollar strengthened on haven bids as Asian markets were hit by the AI rout (Reuters)  
  - VIX and fear gauges surged (largest moves since April reported); gold rose on improved risk sentiment (WSJ/Reuters)  
  - US Treasury yields reacted to jobs data and tech flight; Apple completed a $7.5bn bond sale (Reuters)

Macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a volatile, risk‑off regime driven by a rapid unwind of the AI/data‑centre growth narrative, a simultaneous re‑acceleration of central‑bank tightening and sticky inflation, and heightened political/regulatory uncertainty after the US midterms. Markets are rotating into safe havens and credit scrutiny (especially in bespoke, leveraged sectors like data centres), leaving equity leadership in flux and raising the bar for macro and corporate earnings stability.
