1) AI-driven demand is re-rating large tech, chipmakers and data‑centre economics while catalysing new funding and corporate adoption.
   - Nvidia: forecasts 70% sales growth; reported a blowout quarter and a $442bn market‑value day; paused some revenue‑sharing deals
   - Anthropic: $45bn AI data‑centre deal with Nscale; won court blocking of Pentagon blacklisting; launched an AI tool to run experiments; but its top model “struggles to attract users”
   - Cisco: rolled out personal AI agents to 90,000 employees
   - Investment flows: new VC/industry funds and supply‑chain plays targeting AI bottlenecks

2) Rates and the bond market are unsettled by a hawkish shift in policy rhetoric, pushing short yields higher and rekindling rate‑hike bets.
   - Fed signals: Warsh Jackson Hole comments increased rate‑hike odds
   - Market moves: 2‑year Treasury yields jumped; gold fell ~3%; dollar strengthened and was set for a weekly gain
   - Auctions/yields: 7‑year Treasuries sold at yields near a two‑year high; commentary that ~4.75% is a “natural fit” for the 10‑year

3) Fiscal/Treasury vs market players and the Fed: active Treasury and activist investor moves are creating policy and market friction.
   - Treasury: has taken steps to try to cap long‑term yields
   - Activist/intervention: Scott Bessent’s bond actions triggered high‑profile debate and a reported collision course with the Fed

4) Geopolitical escalation around Iran and Venezuela is injecting real economic leverage into energy and trade flows.
   - Iran war: six months of conflict; shipping via Strait of Hormuz slipped below a 10‑day average; Iran navy claimed “full control”
   - U.S. actions: sanctions on banks; Treasury moved against Egyptian bank branches/UAE ties; U.S. announced intent to take control of ~65bn barrels of Venezuelan oil

5) Inflation and corporate returns diverge: corporate profits are at record highs even as parts of the consumer show strain.
   - Corporate: profits surged to record levels; buybacks remain an active force
   - Labour/data: worker payouts are lagging corporate gains; US payroll revisions showed 79,000 fewer jobs than previously reported in one revision

6) Food, climate and disaster risks are materially impacting supply and price stability.
   - El Niño: flagged as a looming threat to the global food system
   - Himalayan disaster: Nepal floods — death toll reported at 469; more than 1,400 missing; over 90,000 affected
   - Commodities: corn and wheat hit their highest prices in more than three years

7) China and memory/semiconductor competition is intensifying, reshaping supply chains and geopolitics.
   - Chinese memory: YMTC aiming to overtake Samsung and SK Hynix in flash memory race
   - Industrial data: China’s factory activity seen contracting again in August; Chinese firms releasing open‑source AI models

8) Regulatory, legal and corporate‑governance developments are producing large headline liabilities and structural change.
   - Big tech/legal: Meta settlement up to ~$18bn; Alphabet agreed to pay ~£260mn to settle a UK class action
   - Financial regulation: US bank regulators narrowing enforcement focus; BIS comments that stablecoins aren’t credible at scale
   - Markets/regulatory rulings: prediction‑market oversight and court decisions evolving (Kalshi/other cases)

9) Cross‑asset flows are rotating: foreign fixed income and selective EM are drawing attention while some crypto and thematic plays unwind.
   - Flows: foreign bonds have outperformed US fixed income in 2026; global equity funds ended a 13‑week inflow streak
   - Crypto/treasury strategies: Bitcoin treasury companies lost about $80bn in value as business models unwound
   - FX intervention: Japan spent roughly $96.5–$98.7bn (reported) to support the yen in the month

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in an AI‑fuelled growth/reflexivity regime that is boosting certain tech earnings and capex while concentrating demand in semiconductors and data centres, against a backdrop of higher‑for‑longer rate risk after a hawkish pivot at Jackson Hole. Elevated geopolitical tail risks (Middle East, Venezuela) and climate/disaster shocks are amplifying commodity and supply‑chain volatility, leaving markets sensitive to policy moves and cross‑asset flow rotations.
