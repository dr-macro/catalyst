1. Rapid, capital‑intensive AI boom is creating huge financing flows while exposing legal, insurance and cyber risks.  
- Anthropic IPO prospectus warns of “existential risks to humanity”  
- Wall Street banks launch record $60bn chip deal for Broadcom and Anthropic  
- Nvidia $20bn licensing deal with Groq faces lawsuit from engineers  
- SpaceX seeks ~$40bn to buy Nvidia GPUs (major financing talks)  
- Lambda raising $4bn ahead of IPO; RobCo valued at $1bn  
- OpenAI uncovers dozens of hacks; insurance/claims may test Altman/Amodei liability  
- AI agents could cost banks up to $500bn (deposit/competition risk)  

2. Global bond‑market repricing is pushing yields sharply higher and repricing central‑bank paths.  
- Biggest quarterly surge in Treasury yields since 1994  
- US Treasury borrowing costs highest since 2000; 10‑year hit multidecade (24‑yr) highs before easing  
- Strong 10‑year Treasury auction helped calm markets but stress persists  
- ECB path being repriced; “bond vigilantes” headline risk resurfaces  

3. Rising rates and credit costs are blowing up leveraged transactions and commercial‑real‑estate deals.  
- Paramount/Warner leveraged deal ~$110–111bn (timing criticized)  
- Commercial real‑estate deals under strain; WSJ: “Surge in rates is blowing up CRE deals”  
- Skydance, other PE portfolios highlighted for heavy debt exposure  
- Schneider Electric to buy PTC for $23.7bn; McKesson & CD&R $5.8bn; Boots takeover $8.9bn — big-ticket deals in a high‑rate world  

4. Middle East hostilities and Houthi attacks are tightening tanker capacity and lifting oil/refining margins.  
- Attacks on tankers in Strait of Hormuz hit highest weekly level since the war began  
- Supertanker charter to China for $76m (≈10x pre‑war level); captains paid $100,000/month; sailors offered up to $25,000/trip  
- Vitol warns risk of $200/bbl oil; Shell refining margins at record highs  
- US oil trader taking $2bn tanker exposure; oil prices up on supply‑risk headlines  

5. Geopolitical and military escalation is rising across multiple theatres, increasing tail‑risk for trade and markets.  
- Putin ordered military to abandon rules of war; Russian strike killed 11 in one attack  
- Houthis struck Riyadh and Aden and two airports; Iran said it may block “illegal routes” in Hormuz  
- Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey triggered a mutual defence pact  
- US withdrew bombers from UK base after Iranian drone threat  

6. Market leadership is highly concentrated in AI/semiconductor winners while macro stress hits broad sectors.  
- S&P 500 hits record high even as bond market slumps  
- AI boom forecasts US corporate earnings growth ~25% in Q3  
- Samsung forecasts record Q3 profit ~$80bn; chip demand driving massive corporate capex and debt plans  

7. Fiscal and regulatory pressures are mounting in developed markets as higher rates collide with big government spending.  
- French central bank head warns country at risk of being “strangled by interest rates”; France weighs issuing more short‑term debt  
- IMF urges governments to rein in spending, citing energy shock, rising debt and AI risks  
- Regulators increasing scrutiny: probes of insurers, asset managers and tech (multiple investigations and legal actions)

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a regime of sharply higher real yields and credit premia that are compressing leveraged financing while equities remain narrowly led by AI/semiconductor winners — a classic bifurcation. Elevated geopolitical and energy tail risks add inflation and liquidity premiums, leaving policy makers, borrowers and large tech firms wrestling with expensive capital and concentrated market positioning.
