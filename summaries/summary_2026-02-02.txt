1) Fed regime shift and market repricing around Kevin Warsh’s nomination.  
- Kevin Warsh nominated Fed chair (30 Jan 2026)  
- Fed held rates steady (first pause since July)  
- Warsh signals desire to shrink Fed balance sheet  
- Dollar jumped by the most since May 2025 after the nomination  
- Long-term yields rose while short‑term yields eased in the immediate market reaction

2) Precious‑metals rout and collateral market spill‑over.  
- Silver plunged ~31% in a single session (historic selloff)  
- Gold fell ~5%, down nearly $1,000 from peak; Comex gold closed $4,713.90 (end‑month)  
- Reports of a ~$7 trillion hit across gold/silver positions  
- Large banks still forecasting upside (JPMorgan $6,300/oz year‑end target)

3) AI and big‑tech funding frenzy, heavy capex and corporate stress.  
- OpenAI in talks to raise about $40bn; Nvidia/OpenAI $100bn megadeal put on ice  
- Anthropic fundraising doubled to $20bn  
- Oracle planning to raise $45–50bn for AI infrastructure; $25bn bond deal executed

- Amazon to cut ~16,000 corporate jobs and close Amazon Go/Fresh stores  
- Apple bought Q.AI (~$2bn); Tesla to invest $2bn in xAI; SpaceX acquires xAI; Waymo raises ~$16bn at ~$110–126bn valuation

4) Commodities, energy and resource‑security realignments.  
- Oil fell >6% as US‑Iran de‑escalation signalled lower near‑term risk  
- OPEC+ agreed to keep output unchanged (pause)  
- US natural gas hit highest levels in three years after a winter storm, then eased as weather warmed  
- Venezuela exports rebounded to ~800,000 bpd (January) under US control  
- Devon + Coterra combination to create a ~$58bn US shale group  
- US plans $12bn critical‑minerals stockpile; Abu Dhabi folds ~$263bn of assets into a new wealth fund

5) Geopolitical tensions remain a key swing factor but signs of de‑escalation appear.  
- US‑Iran talks expected; Iran delegated emergency powers amid heightened rhetoric  
- Gulf allies signalled limits to military support for a potential US strike on Iran  
- US links security guarantees for Ukraine to a peace framework involving territorial concessions  
- Diplomatic moves: trilateral Ukraine talks set; Rafah crossing reopening; regional mediations active

6) Volatility, risk‑off flows and crypto stress amplify market dislocations.  
- Asian markets plunged (KOSPI ~5% drop; Indonesian bourse down ~5% after an ~$80bn rout)  
- Bitcoin fell below ~$75–80k; crypto liquidations around $2.5bn during the selloff  
- Hedge funds trimming North America exposure amid trade/dollar/megacap weakness  
- US long‑term unemployment at a four‑year high; jobs report delayed by partial government shutdown

7) Active fiscal and industrial policy moves in EMs and resource strategies.  
- India lifts infrastructure spending 11.4% to 12.2 trillion rupees and plans to borrow 17.2 trillion rupees (FY27)  
- India doubles GIFT City tax holiday to 20 years and cuts tariffs to bolster manufacturing  
- Trump administration announces tariffs/stockpile/minerals moves (US to lower some tariffs on India to 18%)  
- Europe pushing on trade and industrial resilience (EU‑India deal developments; strategic minerals concerns)

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a higher‑volatility, policy‑transition regime: markets are re‑pricing for a more conviction‑driven Fed under Kevin Warsh (higher real yields / stronger dollar in the short run) while an AI‑funding boom and corporate capex are concentrating risk in tech and funding markets. Commodity and geopolitical shocks (precious metals, oil, gas, minerals, Iran/Ukraine) are producing episodic stress and safe‑haven rotations that amplify cross‑asset spillovers.
