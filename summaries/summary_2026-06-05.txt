3. The bond market is reasserting influence as real yields climb, liquidity tightens and rate-hike odds shift higher.  
   - Real yields near 20‑year highs  
   - Treasury borrowing accelerating → liquidity beginning to tighten  
   - Investors raise odds of Fed hikes after payrolls; futures lift December hike probability

4. Strong US labour prints are pushing market expectations for monetary policy higher despite mixed growth signals elsewhere.  
   - US nonfarm payrolls (May): +172,000 (surprise); unemployment 4.3%  
   - US job strength lifted yields and dollar; Fed signalling revamp under Kevin Warsh

5. Middle East conflict and Iran-related risks are drawing oil inventories down and forcing re-routing of energy infrastructure.  
   - US oil stocks at lowest since 2004; global oil inventories described as depleted  
   - Iranian oil exports at six‑year low; Oman Mina al Fahal loading suspended after explosion  
   - Gulf players planning pipelines to bypass Strait of Hormuz; Trafigura warns oil at an “inflection point”

6. Credit and private markets show liquidity stress as redemptions spike and managers impose limits.  
   - Blackstone caps withdrawals from flagship private‑credit fund; investors asked to pull $4.4bn  
   - Partners Group prepares/limits withdrawals at funds; Swiss private-equity/Partners Group under pressure

7. Governments and regulators are increasingly active — intervening in AI, reshaping trade policy, and adjusting fiscal/monetary levers.  
   - US officials discussing taking equity stakes in AI firms; administration signals interest in government stakes  
   - EU broadening import quotas/tariffs vs China; China tightening oversight of private funds  
   - India scraps capital gains tax for foreign gov‑bond investors; RBI holds rates at 5.25%

8. China’s tech and geopolitical moves are accelerating strategic competition and supply‑chain realignment.  
   - China overhauls surveillance with advanced AI; Tencent advancing AI agent; Xi to visit North Korea  
   - Europe flags China industrial expansion in Morocco; EU considers trade countermeasures

9. AI infrastructure buildout is straining energy/grid capacity and changing data‑centre economics.  
   - Data‑centre build‑out falling behind schedule; Texas grid flagged after voltage tests fail for AI/data sites  
   - AirTrunk to invest $30bn in India by 2030; SpaceX granted tax exemption for $55bn AI chip plant

10. Macro backdrop is uneven: pockets of strong growth and hiring in the US sit alongside regionally weak activity and new inflation shocks (“spikeflation”).  
   - India GDP +7.8% (Q1); Eurozone retail sales down; Ireland drove Eurozone into contraction in Q1  
   - Commentaries warning of ‘spikeflation’ and energy-driven inflation risks

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
Higher-for-longer rates and rising real yields are raising the cost of capital while liquidity is tightening as fiscal and corporate financing needs increase; markets are bifurcated — concentrated gains in AI/mega‑cap and IPO-related structures amid episodic volatility (notably in semis) and stress in private credit; geopolitics (Middle East, China) is the primary tail risk, keeping energy and inflation upside probabilities elevated.
