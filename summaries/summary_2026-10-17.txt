1) Geopolitical shocks are driving energy supply risk and insurance/security costs.
- Oil/Brent touched ~$110 a barrel (first time since May).
- Houthis attacked Saudi Aramco headquarters; Houthis encircle new targets.
- Two cargo ships sank near the Strait of Hormuz; captains paid $100,000/month to transit.
- Saudi events delayed after Houthi attacks.

2) Russia–Ukraine war escalation is disrupting military aid coordination and critical infrastructure.
- Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian refineries; Russia claims hits on steel plants and power infrastructure.
- US threatened to block arms for Ukraine over refinery attacks; US floated unfreezing some Russian assets to restart talks.
- Starlink restrictions hit Ukrainian operations; Zelenskyy publicly critical.
- Political friction: Trump told Ukraine to “get a new leader.”

3) AI boom met by a financing and security reckoning that is seeding market stress.
- CoreWeave: bonds in distress; stock and bond set for weekly plunge; CEO sold nearly $1bn of stock.
- Investors dumping data‑centre bonds; data‑centre IPO debacle sparked a market meltdown.
- Anthropic: quarterly revenue up nearly 50% to $16.8bn; had highest junk credit rating in June; offers Blackstone double‑digit yield; signs lease for Canadian data centre.
- OpenAI security incident: employee arrested/accused of bugging, one‑way ticket to Saudi Arabia; computing chief departing.
- G7 plans simulated AI attacks on banks; IMF and regulators warn of AI risks.

4) The bond market is repricing higher real yields and volatility, lifting long‑end rates into 5%+ territory.
- Multiple notes flag 10‑year Treasury at or heading toward 5%–6% (articles: “Bonds at 5%”, “Rising real yields point to a 6% 10‑Year”).
- Commentary: don’t expect a break back below 5%; Treasury auction commentary and shelter flows.
- Treasuries rallied at times as investors sought shelter from AI turbulence.

5) Energy‑driven inflation is forcing central banks and markets to reassess the rate outlook.
- US headline inflation reported at 3.7% (September).
- Bank of Canada sees sharply higher inflation outlook as oil surges; BOJ/IMF warn tightening may be needed if inflation overshoots.
- Fed commentary mixed: some officials withholding forward signals; markets split on a near‑term hold vs. further hikes.

6) Tech and AI sector volatility is prompting sector rotation and wider market risk‑off.
- Chip and Big Tech stocks suffered the biggest weekly drops in some measures; chip stocks slid after Broadcom boss called the space a “casino.”
- Market commentary: risk of an AI crunch for stocks rising; investors predicting new record price for gold within two years (flight to alternatives).

7) Credit, regulatory and legal shocks are amplifying market fragility.
- Bank of America fined $1.2bn for money‑laundering failures.
- Credit Suisse secured $800mn from insurers in a Greensill settlement.
- US annual budget gap near $2tn; US solicited exchange of up to 17 million barrels from SPR.

8) Political volatility and policy shifts are increasing tail‑risk to trade, aid and capital flows.
- Trump waived/exempted key Russia‑related sanctions and tariffs under new law; other actions (sanctions waivers, Starlink limits) complicate allied cohesion.
- European politics: Le Pen questions EU funding for Ukraine; France/Germany negotiating trade/auto deals.
- Emerging‑market stress: Indonesia stocks plunged after MSCI flagged potential index exclusion; Asian bonds saw largest monthly foreign outflow since March 2020.

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a higher‑volatility, commodity‑inflation regime driven by geopolitical shocks (Middle East, Russia‑Ukraine) that is forcing a repricing of real yields (10‑year yields in the 5%+ area) and stressing credit in concentrated pockets (AI/data‑centre borrowers). Market behaviour is bifurcated: defensive demand for sovereigns and gold coexists with sector‑specific risk‑offs in tech/credit, leaving policy uncertainty and tail‑risks elevated.
