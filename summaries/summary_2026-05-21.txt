2) Bond markets are repricing rapidly as sticky inflation and the energy shock push real yields and term premiums materially higher.
   - Real yields near 20‑year highs
   - Treasury premium climbing again
   - Treasury yields resumed climb; Fed minutes show more officials open to a rate hike
   - Warnings of instability: “rates face risk of instability” / “bond rout accelerating”

5) Commodity and supply‑chain stresses are broadening beyond crude into food, fertiliser, shipping and critical minerals, raising near‑term price and scarcity risks.

6) Labour, corporate restructuring and policy responses show uneven adjustment to AI and cost shocks, fueling pockets of wage pressure, strikes and re‑skilling.
   - Samsung: union suspended strike after tentative wage deal; big bonuses reported (~$416,000 mentioned)
   - Big Four: more job ads for AI specialists than auditors
   - Major tech and services layoffs/reshuffles (Meta, Intuit, others)
   - Standard Chartered: plan to replace “lower‑value human capital” with AI comment and damage control
   - India considering rate hikes as rupee slumps; Bank Indonesia delivered first rate hike in years

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines):
We are in a geopolitically driven, inflation‑sensitive regime: an energy shock from the Middle East is colliding with a concentrated AI/tech rally, forcing a sharp repricing of rates and risk premia while creating pronounced market bifurcation (big‑tech/AI winners versus cyclical and consumer‑exposed losers). Expect elevated volatility, higher real yields, and policy‑driven interventions in strategic sectors.
