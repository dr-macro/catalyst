1) Global bond sell-off and hawkish central bank repricing are driving yields sharply higher.
- 10-year US Treasury yield "eyes" 5%
- Bond yields at 2‑decade highs; UK yields hit fresh 19‑year high
- Japan borrowing costs at 30‑year high; yen under pressure after Warsh comments
- ECB hawkishness weighing on French government bonds; calls for further rate rises
- Eurozone inflation 3.3% in August
- Public debt concerns as US debt tops $40 trillion

2) Middle East kinetic escalation is elevating oil risk and disrupting shipping routes.
- US and Iran exchanged fire for first time in >1 month; US launched further strikes
- Saudi: Iran attacked vessel in Hormuz, killing two sailors
- Oil supertankers hit in the Strait of Hormuz; shipping traffic below 10‑day average
- Energy flows: >17 million barrels transited Hormuz on one day; Iran blacklisted more ships

3) AI-led earnings surge and data‑centre investment are concentrating corporate profits and capex.
- Big Tech recorded a $160bn profit boost from gains on stakes in other AI companies
- US corporate profits surged to record levels while worker payouts lag
- Anthropic signed an exclusive cloud deal worth ~$35bn backed by Nvidia
- Dell, HPE, Snowflake and Broadcom raised forecasts / flagged strong AI-server and AI-cloud demand
- National data‑centre projects consolidating US AI lead

4) AI is simultaneously accelerating restructuring: cost cuts, reskilling and concentration in services.
- Junior consultants being pulled back to office as AI increases demand for human skills
- Consultancies and Big Law pushing cuts/fee pressure because of AI
- WPP cutting up to 1,000 roles
- Ernst & Young handing $100m in bonuses tied to 'human' skills

5) Energy dealmaking and repositioning (including Venezuela) are reshaping upstream investment flows.
- Chevron pledged $7bn to double Venezuela production over five years
- Trump administration pushing large Venezuela oil deals (coverage cites a 65bn‑barrel figure)
- Scramble for gas assets driving dealmaking to decade highs; European gas at three‑year highs

6) Geopolitical/industrial rivalry with China is intensifying across tech, trade and diplomacy.
- China warned the US it could retaliate over Iran sanctions and derailed G20 consensus
- YMTC targeting leadership in flash memory vs Samsung and SK Hynix
- Xi Jinping made a high‑profile Egypt visit ahead of a Trump summit
- South Korean FX authorities bought ~$20bn in repatriated SK Hynix funds

7) Markets are bifurcating: AI winners and data‑centre beneficiaries outperform while broader risk premia widen.
- Foreign bonds and certain international equities leading over US fixed income in 2026
- Investors rotating into AI beneficiaries (Snowflake, Nvidia, HPE), even as broader bond sell‑off pressures markets
- Oil and energy price moves are feeding through into higher yields and consumer cost worries

Current macro/markets regime (2–3 lines)
We are in a higher‑for‑longer interest‑rate regime driven by sticky inflation, hawkish central banks and a global bond repricing amplified by energy and geopolitical risk; concurrently, AI-led profit concentration and heavy data‑centre capex are creating a narrow equity leadership that contrasts with rising market breadth and sovereign funding stress. Expect elevated volatility, commodity sensitivity, and persistent dispersion between AI/tech winners and the rest of the market.
